## Overview

In this project, students will work in **pairs** to develop a series of **four digital compositions exploring different visual interpretations of an assigned animal**.

> **The assigned animal will be announced in class. Animal selection will take place through a randomized draw.**

Each student will create **two individual compositions**:

1. **One abstract interpretation**
2. **One realistic or figurative interpretation**

Working from research and sketching through digital production and final print preparation, students will explore how the same subject can be transformed through different approaches to **form, composition, colour, image-making, visual hierarchy, and representation**.

> This project combines **collaborative planning with individual execution**. Both students are responsible for ensuring that the four compositions function together as a visually cohesive series. Follow all document setup, production, submission, and exhibition requirements outlined in each section.

## Goal

Create a cohesive five-image series that combines four individual interpretations of an assigned animal with one photographic reference. Develop the work through research, sketching, digital production, revision, printing, exhibition, and documentation.

## Software

- **Adobe InDesign** — planning and project documentation
- **Adobe Illustrator** — vector-based composition and illustration
- **Adobe Photoshop** — raster-based composition, image manipulation, collage, texture, and digital painting

> You may use **Illustrator, Photoshop, or a combination of both** to create the final compositions. Choose the software according to the visual approach and techniques required by your design.

> **Do not use Image Trace in Adobe Illustrator or automated tracing techniques to generate the compositions.** 

## Accessibility and learning support

- The written instructions contain the required steps. Demonstrations and embedded tutorials provide additional support.
- Partners may divide research, planning, file preparation, printing, installation, documentation, and visitor-engagement responsibilities according to their strengths and access needs. Each student remains responsible for creating two individual compositions.
- You may use zoom, screen magnification, keyboard navigation, adapted input devices, colour-identification tools, or other assistive technology.
- Do not rely on colour alone to distinguish information or connect the series. Use labels, numerical values, contrast, shape, texture, placement, or another visible cue.
- If photography, printing, mounting, installation, speaking with visitors, or another required process creates an access barrier, contact the instructor early to arrange an equivalent role or method that meets the same learning outcomes.
- Make planning and documentation PDFs text-based where possible, with readable type, strong contrast, descriptive headings, a logical reading order, captions, and image descriptions.
