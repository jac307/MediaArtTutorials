# Composition Techniques I – Part 1

![Examples from previous Composition Techniques I activities](imgs/01.jpg)

**Goal:** Design and create **four black-and-white vector compositions** by building, repeating, and systematically altering a super-unit.

Each composition must combine:

- **One assigned Interrelationship of Form**
- **One assigned principle from Wucius Wong**
- **One spatial focus adapted from Timothy Samara’s “Form and Space”**
- **One grid structure**
- **One super-unit repeated and altered across the complete composition**

> **A super-unit is the starting module, not the final composition.**  
> Creating one super-unit and placing it once in the centre of the page does not complete the activity. Each final composition must contain **at least eight repeated instances** of its super-unit. Those instances must be altered through controlled changes in scale, rotation, orientation, spacing, position, proportion, or positive–negative relationships.

## Activity overview

- **Outputs:** Four planning sketches and four digital compositions
- **Interrelationships:** Detached, Touching, Overlapped, and Interpenetration
- **Wong principles:** Repetition, Structure, Similarity, and Gradation
- **Samara focuses:** Interval and tension, Figure/Ground, Depth and hierarchy, and Movement and direction
- **Grid structures:** Inactive, Active, Visible, and Repetition-based
- **Shapes:** Geometric shapes only
- **Colour:** Black and white only
- **Software:** Adobe Illustrator
- **Format:** 1000 × 1000 px
- **Submission:** One sketch PDF and four composition PDFs

## Activity stages

Complete the following stages in order.

## Interrelationships of Form

![Detached, Touching, Overlapped, and Interpenetration](imgs/02.png)

Each composition begins with a super-unit that clearly demonstrates one assigned relationship:

- **Detached:** The forms remain separate.
- **Touching:** The forms meet at an edge or point without overlapping.
- **Overlapped:** One form appears in front of another.
- **Interpenetration:** Overlapping forms remain visible through each other, producing shared internal areas.

## Super-unit versus composition

A **super-unit** is a small structure created by combining two or more shapes through an Interrelationship of Form.

The final composition must develop that starting structure into a larger visual system:

1. Create one clearly resolved super-unit.
2. Repeat the super-unit at least eight times.
3. Alter the repeated instances in a controlled way.
4. Organize the instances through the assigned grid and principle.
5. Use the complete square format, including its edges and negative spaces.

The relationship inside the original super-unit must remain identifiable after the instances are altered.

![Examples of super-units](imgs/03.png)

## Grid structures

- Inactive grid

## Form and space

The activity also applies concepts adapted from Timothy Samara’s chapter “Form and Space”:

- **Interval and tension:** Distance affects whether forms appear connected, separated, stable, or active.
- **Figure/Ground:** Positive forms and negative spaces must be designed together.
- **Depth and hierarchy:** Overlap, scale, placement, and value affect which forms advance or recede.
- **Movement and direction:** Repeated changes in scale, position, spacing, and orientation can guide the viewer’s eye.

> **Source:** Samara, T. (2007). “Form and Space.” In *Design Elements: A Graphic Style Manual*. Rockport Publishers.

Create all four compositions:

| Composition | Interrelationship of Form | Wong principle | Samara spatial focus | Required visual direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Detached** | **Repetition** | **Interval and tension** | Repeat the detached super-unit at least eight times. Control the distances between instances so some relationships feel stable while others create tension. |
| **2** | **Touching** | **Structure** | **Figure/Ground** | Repeat the touching super-unit at least eight times within a clear structure. Use shared edges, placement, and negative space to make both the positive forms and the surrounding spaces active. |
| **3** | **Overlapped** | **Similarity** | **Depth and hierarchy** | Repeat the overlapped super-unit at least eight times. Maintain visual similarity while altering scale, placement, or orientation to create foreground, middle ground, and background. |
| **4** | **Interpenetration** | **Gradation** | **Movement and direction** | Repeat the interpenetrating super-unit at least eight times. Use gradual changes in scale, proportion, orientation, spacing, or position to guide the eye through the composition. |

## Grid requirement

Assign one different grid structure to each composition:

- Inactive
- Active
- Visible
- Repetition-based

Use every grid structure once. Select the grid that best supports each required combination.

## Variation requirement

Within each composition, alter the repeated super-unit through at least **three** of the following variables:

- Scale
- Rotation
- Orientation
- Position
- Spacing
- Proportion
- Cropping at the format edge
- Positive–negative reversal

The alterations must create an intentional visual system. Do not scatter copies randomly.

## Develop the full composition

For each file:

1. Repeat the super-unit at least eight times.
2. Alter the instances through at least three controlled variables.
3. Use the selected grid to organize the changes.
4. Maintain the assigned Interrelationship of Form within the altered instances.
5. Use the assigned Wong principle to organize the larger system.
6. Apply the assigned Samara spatial focus.
7. Consider the relationship between the composition and the square format.
8. Refine visual balance with your eyes rather than relying only on mathematical alignment.

> The repeated instances must interact as a composition. A single completed super-unit, a row of identical copies, or copies placed without a clear spatial system will not meet the activity requirements.
