The final submission is divided into **two parts**:

1. **In-Person Submission – Assembly & Class Exhibition**
2. **Online Submission – Final Project Documentation on Avenue to Learn**

Your final grade will consider the complete development of the project, including:

- Application of concepts from **Composition Techniques I and II**

- Laser-cutting and engraving quality
- Structural assembly
- Exhibition presentation
- Quality and consistency of the final documentation

## Complete the painting

Before assembling the sculpture, complete the painting of all planes, the base, and any additional structural components.

Follow the **approved colour palette** from Part 2.

### Painting requirements

- Use the approved **hand-mixed colours**.
- Apply a **white base coat** before the final colours where required.
- Apply **2–3 thin, even coats**.
- Allow each coat to dry completely.
- Keep brush strokes smooth and consistent.
- Paint all visible surfaces and edges.
- Maintain the planned colour relationships and progression.
- Preserve the visibility of intentional engraved textures.

> Visible brush strokes, inconsistent coverage, unintended colour changes, or unfinished surfaces may result in **grade deductions**.

## Assemble the sculpture

Once all components are fully painted and dry:

- Assemble the sculpture according to the approved design.
- Maintain the planned spacing, layering, direction, and structural relationships.
- Use **minimal glue**.
- Make sure all connections are secure.
- Keep the base and structural components clean.
- Remove or avoid visible glue marks.
- Check the sculpture from multiple perspectives before considering it complete.

> The final assembly should reflect the structural and compositional decisions developed during Parts 1 and 2.

## Class Exhibition

Your group must bring the **fully completed sculpture to the final class exhibition**.

The sculpture must be:

- Fully painted
- Fully assembled
- Completely dry
- Structurally stable
- Ready to exhibit when brought to class

> **Bringing the completed sculpture to the final class exhibition is a required part of the project. Failure to bring the work to the exhibition, or bringing an incomplete project, will result in a grade deduction.**

During the exhibition, the instructor will evaluate the physical work, including:

- Craftsmanship
- Colour application
- Colour relationships and proportioning
- Engraved texture
- Structural organization
- Assembly quality
- Stability
- Overall presentation

## 1. Create a title page

Create a **title page at the beginning of the document** using **two professional photographs** of the completed sculpture from **two distinctive perspectives**.

The title page must include:

- **Title of the work**
- **Group members' names**
- **Two professional photographs of the sculpture**

The photographs may show, for example:

- Front and side
- Front and three-quarter view
- Low and elevated perspective
- Two views that clearly reveal different spatial relationships within the sculpture

The photographs should document the **sculpture itself rather than the exhibition environment**. Use a **plain, neutral background** so the form, colour, texture, and structure of the sculpture remain the focus.

> The two images should be arranged as part of a **visually cohesive title-page design** and should establish the visual identity of the final documentation.

### Photography requirements

- [Available Equipment](Equipment.md)
- [DSLR Camera Introduction to Camera Parts and Settings](DSRL-Camera.md)

Consider consistency across both photographs:

|  |  |  |
|---|---|---|
| Lighting | Background | Framing |
| Camera angle | Image quality | Colour consistency |
| Focus | Scale | Visual presentation |

The photographs should:

- Be sharp and properly exposed.
- Use consistent lighting and colour.
- Avoid distracting backgrounds.
- Present the sculpture clearly.
- Show the form, colour, engraved texture, depth, and assembly.
- Be edited consistently if image adjustments are necessary.

---

## 2. Add the final scanned colour palette

Under the existing **Colour** section, add the **final approved version of the scanned physical colour palette**.

If your group revised the palette after instructor feedback, include the **final revised palette**, not the earlier version.

> The scan should be clearly presented and large enough to evaluate the painted colours.

---

## 3. Add an Exhibition Documentation page

Create a separate page documenting the sculpture as it appeared in the **Class Exhibition**.

The page must include:

- **Name of the exhibition**
- **Date**
- **Location**
- **3–4 photographs of the sculpture in the exhibition context**

The exhibition photographs should show the relationship between the sculpture and its display environment.

Possible images may include:

- Full view of the installed sculpture
- Wider view showing the exhibition space
- Different perspective of the work in context
- Installation or display detail

## Image size and layout

All photographs must be large enough to evaluate clearly.

- The **largest images should occupy approximately 1/3 of a page**.
- The **smallest images should occupy approximately 1/4 of a page**.
- Do not use very small thumbnail images.
- Maintain consistent spacing and alignment.
- Keep image proportions intact.
- Do not stretch or distort photographs.
- Design each page so the photographs and written information form a **visually cohesive composition**.

> Consider the final PDF as a piece of **professional project documentation**, not simply a collection of images.
