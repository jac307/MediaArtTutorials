# Live Foley

## Group project · Groups of four

![Examples from the Live Foley activity](imgs/09.png)

## Goal

Create a **30-second live Foley performance** using **three cameras and multiple audio-recording devices**.

The focus of this activity is coordinating live sound performance with multi-camera recording, microphone placement, lighting, synchronization, and post-production.

During post-production, you will combine the three camera perspectives and three live audio perspectives into one audiovisual work. The assigned silent-film clip will remain visible using picture-in-picture.

> Attendance, participation, and active engagement during class activities are part of the project rubric.

## Project overview

- **Format:** 30-second multi-camera Live Foley video
- **Performance:** Only sounds produced live during recording
- **Cameras:** Three-camera setup on tripods
- **Audio:** Shotgun microphone, condenser microphone, and ambient stereo recorder
- **Lighting:** Three-point lighting adapted to the Foley station
- **Location:** Assigned workstations in Togo Salmon Hall
- **Production time:** During class
- **Collaboration:** Work in station groups for setup and support; submit in groups of four
- **Image orientation:** Landscape
- **Post-production:** Adobe Premiere Pro

## Required camera settings

- **Shooting mode:** Manual (`M`) mode
- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps
- **Shutter speed:** `1/60`
- **White balance:** Custom White Balance (`CWB`)
- **Focus:** Manual Focus (`MF`)
- **Metering:** Evaluative or Matrix
- **Image Stabilization:** Off
- **Aperture:** Manual
- **ISO:** Manual; do not use Auto ISO

## Assign group roles

Each project group consists of four students:

- **Three students** perform the Live Foley sounds.
- **One student** monitors the cameras and audio recorders.
- Other students in the same station can also support each group's recordings. 

Roles may change during rehearsal, but the final responsibilities must be established before recording.

## Plan the sound actions

All sounds must be produced live during the recorded performance.

Possible materials and actions include:

- Fabric, paper, plastic, wood, glass, and metal
- Footsteps / Shoes
- Scraping
- Tapping
- Rubbing
- Crumpling
- Impacts
- Mechanical sounds
- Subtle textures
- Louder percussive sounds

You may use everyday objects, handmade Foley devices, adapted materials, or musical instruments.

**All objects and instruments must be:**

- **Brought by your group**
- Safe to use in the production space
- Positioned within comfortable reach
- Quiet to handle between intended sound actions
- Resettable between takes

> No music, stock audio, sound libraries, or sounds recorded after the production session may be added during post-production.

## Create the brainstorming document

Create a planning document that identifies:

- The selected 30-second time frame
- Each required sound
- The object or instrument used to create each sound
- The physical action used to produce it
- The performer responsible for each sound
- The student responsible for technical monitoring
- The objects and materials each student must bring
- Anticipated timing, coordination, or volume challenges

The document does not have a fixed visual format. Use a table, timeline, annotated screenshot, written outline, or another structure that helps the group plan clearly.

## Submit the brainstorming document

- **Format:** PDF
- **Filename:** `Group-#-Brainstorming.pdf`

Replace `#` with your assigned group number.

## Rehearse before production day

Your group must practise individually and collectively before Thursday.

During rehearsal:

- Watch the selected scene repeatedly.
- Identify every required sound.
- Assign specific sounds to each performer.
- Practise the timing of each action.
- Determine which sounds should be subtle, medium, or loud.
- Coordinate overlapping actions.
- Avoid masking quiet sounds with louder actions.
- Practise the complete sequence without stopping.
- Prepare to perform the scene two or three times during class.

## Establish the Foley working area

Collectively establish the central area where the objects and performers will be positioned.

The group may work:

- Seated behind a table
- Standing behind a table
- On the floor
- Around another safe working surface
- Using another configuration approved by the professor

The arrangement must:

- Keep all objects within reach
- Allow three performers to work comfortably
- Keep sound-producing gestures visible
- Leave space for cameras, microphones, lights, and the monitor
- Keep doors, walkways, exits, and accessibility routes unobstructed
- Work for all three project groups sharing the station

Each project group may reorganize its objects before its recording period.

## Set up the three audio perspectives

For detailed microphone placement, connections, and recording settings, follow the [W5 Live Foley Technical Walkthrough](../TechWalks/TW-W5.md){:target="_blank"}.

| Audio device | Connection or support | Primary role |
|---|---|---|
| **Sennheiser ME 80** | Microphone stand; connected to a ZOOM H4n with an XLR cable | Detail microphone for subtle Foley sounds |
| **Shotgun microphone** | Connected directly to Camera B | Directional sound synchronized with the side camera |
| **ZOOM H4n ambient recorder** | Mounted above or near the centre of the station | Overall stereo recording of the complete Foley performance |

Microphone positions may be adjusted for each project group.

## Set up the three cameras

For detailed camera placement and framing instructions, follow the [W5 Live Foley Technical Walkthrough](../TechWalks/TW-W5.md){:target="_blank"}.

| Camera | Lens | Support | Primary role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Camera A** | Default zoom lens | Tripod | Main front wide shot |
| **Camera B** | Short focal-length or wide-angle lens | Tripod | Side wide shot with shotgun microphone |
| **Camera C** | Default lens or additional ones | Tripod | Back or overhead view |

## Set up the three lights

For detailed light positioning and intensity instructions, follow the [W5 Live Foley Technical Walkthrough](../TechWalks/TW-W5.md){:target="_blank"}.

| Light | Modifier or position | Primary role |
|---|---|---|
| **Fill light** | Softbox; positioned first | Provides broad illumination across most of the Foley station |
| **Key light** | Directed toward the performers | Keeps performers, gestures, and sound-producing actions visible |
| **Back light** | Positioned behind or beside the performers at a lower intensity | Creates subtle separation from the dark background |

> Lighting choices should prioritize the visibility of the objects, hands, gestures, and physical production of sound.

## Position the video monitor

Connect the monitor to the computer containing the assigned film clip.

Position it:

- Within clear view of all performers
- Outside all three camera frames
- Away from microphones and stands
- At a comfortable viewing height
- Where performers do not need to turn away from their Foley objects
- With cables secured outside active walkways

The monitor should remain muted during the final recording.

## Record a complete test run

Record one complete rehearsal using all performers, objects, cameras, microphones, recorders, lights, and the reference monitor.

1. Start both ZOOM H4n recorders.
2. Start Cameras A, B, and C.
3. Confirm verbally that every device is recording.
4. Announce the station or project-group number.
5. Announce that this is a test recording.
6. Perform one clear hand clap within view of all three cameras.
7. Wait approximately **2–3 seconds**.
8. Start the reference video.
9. Perform the complete 30-second Foley sequence.
10. Wait several seconds after the sequence finishes.
11. Stop all cameras and recorders.

Include both the quietest and loudest planned Foley actions.

## Review the audio

Listen to the complete test recordings using headphones.

Check that:

- The Sennheiser ME 80 captures the subtle Foley sounds.
- Camera B receives sound from the connected shotgun microphone.
- The ambient ZOOM H4n captures the overall Foley performance.
- Camera A records clear reference audio for synchronization.
- The synchronization clap is audible on every recording.
- Quiet actions remain audible.
- Loud actions remain below clipping.
- There is no distortion, excessive handling noise, cable noise, or monitor sound.
- Make needed changes, if any.

## Review the camera footage

Check that:

- Camera A shows the complete Foley station.
- Camera B provides a clear side perspective.
- Camera C shows the objects and physical sound-producing actions.
- Important gestures remain within the frames.
- Performers do not block important objects.
- The focus remains consistent.
- The synchronization clap is visible.
- The footage from the three cameras has compatible exposure and colour.
- Make needed changes, if any.

## Review the lighting

Check that:

- The fill light illuminates most of the working area.
- The softbox creates broad and controlled illumination.
- The key light keeps the performers visible.
- The back light creates subtle separation from the background.
- Hands, objects, and working surfaces remain visible.
- Heavy shadows do not conceal important actions.
- Bright or reflective objects are not clipped.
- The manual ISO does not introduce excessive image noise.
- Make needed changes, if any. Don't forget to re-adjust camera setting if you change the lighting.

## Recording time

Each project group will record **two or three complete takes**.

- Organize a clear recording order within the station.
- Each group has approximately **30 minutes**.
- The station group is responsible for managing the schedule.
- While another group records, remain at the station and provide technical and production support.

Each project group is responsible for the quality of its own recordings.

## Record and synchronize each take

1. Start the ZOOM H4n connected to the Sennheiser ME 80.
2. Start the ambient ZOOM H4n.
3. Start Camera A.
4. Start Camera B.
5. Start Camera C.
6. Confirm verbally that every device is recording.
7. Announce the group number.
8. Announce the take number.
9. Perform one clear hand clap within view of all three cameras.
10. Wait approximately **2–3 seconds**.
11. Start the reference video.
12. Perform the complete 30-second Foley sequence.
13. Continue recording for several seconds after the sequence ends.
14. Stop the cameras and recorders.

Do not stop any device during the performance.

The complete scene must be recorded as one continuous take on all cameras and audio recorders.

## Review each take

After every take:

- Confirm that every device created a file.
- Review the synchronization clap.
- Review the beginning and ending.
- Listen briefly to the audio.
- Check for clipping, distortion, or technical failure.
- Check the camera framing and focus.
- Identify performance problems.
- Reset the objects.
- Make only the necessary corrections.

Record at least **two complete takes**. Record a third take when time allows or when the first two contain technical or performance problems.

## Assemble the video in Premiere Pro

Create a sequence using:

- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps
- **Duration:** Exactly 30 seconds

Import:

- Camera A footage
- Camera B footage
- Camera C footage
- The assigned silent-film clip

## Synchronize the cameras

Use the synchronization clap and reference audio to align the three camera recordings.

Confirm that:

- The visible clap occurs on the same frame in all three camera angles.
- The clap waveform aligns across the camera-audio tracks.
- The complete performances remain synchronized.
- The selected take is the same across all three cameras.

During the Premiere Pro edit, use only the audio recorded by the **shotgun microphone connected to Camera B**.

Mute Camera A and Camera C audio after synchronization.

> The shotgun recording is the temporary audio reference for the visual edit. The final sound mix will be completed in REAPER using the two ZOOM H4n recordings.

## Edit the camera perspectives

Use straight cuts or jump cuts between the synchronized camera angles.

The edit should:

- Show how the sounds are physically produced
- Use Camera A to establish the complete performance
- Use Camera B to reveal the side perspective and primary directional sound area
- Use Camera C to emphasize objects, hands, and gestures
- Maintain synchronization with the Foley performance
- Keep important actions visible
- Create a clear visual rhythm

## Add the source clip using picture-in-picture

Place the assigned silent-film clip in one corner of the final frame.

The inset may be positioned:

- Top-left
- Top-right
- Bottom-left
- Bottom-right

The inset must:

- Remain visible throughout the performance
- Preserve the original aspect ratio
- Be large enough for the action to be understood
- Avoid covering important Foley objects or performers
- Remain synchronized with the Live Foley performance

## Add titles and credits

Include:

- Project title
- Group number
- Names of all four students
- Year
- Source-film title
- Source-film director

Titles and credits must be readable and must not obscure important actions.

## Export the picture edit for REAPER

Export the completed picture edit with the synchronized shotgun-microphone reference audio.

- **Format:** MP4
- **Codec:** H.264
- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps

This is an intermediate file used for the final sound mix.

## Complete the sound mix in REAPER

Import:

- The edited Premiere Pro video
- The Sennheiser ME 80 detail recording
- The ambient ZOOM H4n recording

Synchronize both ZOOM recordings with the shotgun-microphone reference track using the synchronization clap.

Use the three live audio perspectives:

- Shotgun microphone connected to Camera B
- Sennheiser ME 80 detail microphone
- Ambient ZOOM H4n recording

Adjust the mix so:

- Subtle sounds remain audible.
- Louder sounds do not overwhelm the mix.
- No source is disproportionately loud or quiet.
- The different sonic layers remain distinguishable.
- The audio supports the visual performance.
- The final mix remains below clipping.
- The dynamic range remains appropriate.

You may:

- Adjust levels
- Apply fades
- Use volume automation
- Pan recordings
- Apply corrective equalization
- Reduce unwanted low-frequency noise
- Normalize or limit the final output appropriately

## Restrictions

- No added music
- No external Foley
- No stock audio
- No sound-library material
- No sounds recorded after the production session
- No footage other than the three camera recordings and assigned source clip
- No decorative video transitions

> All audible material must originate from the Live Foley performance recorded during class.
