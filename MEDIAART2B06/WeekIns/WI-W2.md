# Chiaroscuro Interview

## Pair project

![Examples from the Chiaroscuro Interview activity](imgs/05.png)

## Goal

Create a **30-second interview video** that uses **chiaroscuro lighting** as a compositional and conceptual constraint.

The focus of this activity is **lighting, framing, camera coordination, and audio clarity**, rather than complex narrative development.

> Attendance, participation, and active engagement during class activities are part of the project rubric.

## Project overview

- **Format:** 30-second interview video
- **Lighting:** Chiaroscuro using an adapted three-point lighting setup
- **Cameras:** Three-camera setup
- **Audio** Shotgun and lapel microphones; no music
- **Location:** Assigned workstations in Togo Salmon Hall
- **Production time:** During class
- **Collaboration:** Work in station groups of 10 for setup and support; submit in pairs
- **Image orientation:** Landscape

## Required camera settings

- **Shooting mode:**  Manual (`M`) mode
- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Aspect ratio** 16:9
- **Frame Rate** 30 fps
- **White balance:** Custom White Balance (`CWB`)
- **Focus:** Manual Focus
- **Image Stabilization:** On when handheld; off when using a tripod

## *The Fog of War* (2003), directed by Errol Morris

A controlled example of interview-based chiaroscuro. Low-key lighting separates the subject from the darkened background, while shadows shape the face and reinforce themes of ambiguity, reflection, and moral uncertainty.

- [Watch the trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VgA98V1Ubk8){:target="_blank"}
- [Watch the full documentary](https://watchdocumentaries.com/the-fog-of-war/){:target="_blank"}

## *IWOW: I Walk on Water* (2020), directed by Khalik Allah

An example of extreme chiaroscuro in documentary filmmaking. A strong directional light sculpts faces emerging from darkness, emphasizing presence, intimacy, and vulnerability.

- [Watch the official trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgDVdFBu8FE){:target="_blank"}
- [Watch the extended trailer and excerpts](https://vimeo.com/390915129){:target="_blank"}

> **Content warning:** This work contains the N-word and depicts drug use and mental-health concerns.

## *No Quarto da Vanda* (*In Vanda’s Room*, 2000), directed by Pedro Costa

An influential example of low-light interior documentary filmmaking. Chiaroscuro emerges from minimal available light rather than a conventional studio setup. Faces and bodies appear partially illuminated against deep shadow, creating a sense of intimacy and stillness.

- [Watch the trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMtsdwWSjyk){:target="_blank"}
- [Watch the opening sequence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rp4QXi1a-yQ){:target="_blank"}

> **Content warning:** This film depicts drug use, addiction, and precarious living conditions.

## Select the interviewee

The interviewee should preferably be another student outside your pair, ideally someone from your station group. This allows both members of the pair to concentrate on camera operation, framing, lighting, and audio.

> You are not graded on what the interviewee says. Do not spend excessive time scripting or rehearsing the response.

## Speaking length

- Record approximately **45–60 seconds** of interview content.
- Edit the material into a final video of exactly **30 seconds**.
- You do not need to use all the recorded material.

## Choose one of the following prompts:

1. **Observation:** “Describe a place you pass through often but rarely pay attention to.”
2. **Light and space:** “Describe how light changes a space you know well.”
3. **Presence:** “What helps you feel grounded when you are overwhelmed?”
4. **Time:** “Describe a moment when time felt slow.”

The prompt supports your work with light, framing, camera coordination, and sound. It is not a writing or performance exercise.

> Respond naturally and in your own words. Do not act, memorize a response, or perform a character.

## Plan the visual approach

Use the worksheet to decide:

- The framing and composition for each camera
- The strength of the light–shadow contrast
- Where shadows will fall on the interviewee’s face and body
- Whether the lighting will create a warmer, more intimate tone or a cooler, more neutral tone

[Download the Brainstorming and Sketching Worksheet](imgs/Chiaroscuro_Brainstorming_Worksheet.pdf){:target="_blank"}

When completing a printed worksheet, scan or photograph each page and combine the images into one PDF.

## Submit the worksheet

- **Format:** PDF
- **Filename:** `Group-#-Brainstorming.pdf`

## Set up three-point lighting for chiaroscuro

![Key Light Example of Arragement](../TechWalks/imgs/ThreePointLight.png)

Use the three lights to create strong contrast between light and shadow:

- Key light: Main illumination
- Fill light: Controls shadow depth
- Back light: Separates the interviewee from the background

**Refer to the Week 2 Technical Walkthrough for detailed setup instructions.**

> Handle all lighting equipment carefully. Keep stands stable and organize cables so they do not obstruct doors, walkways, or accessibility routes.

## Set up Multi-Camera Recording and Audio equipment

When recording with multiple cameras, each camera should have a distinct purpose within the overall visual coverage. For this activity, you will follow a standard three-camera setup, using complementary angles and shot types rather than creating three nearly identical images.

![Diagram showing the proposed three-camera interview arrangement](imgs/06.jpg)

| Camera | Lens | Support | Primary role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Camera A** | Default kit lens | Tripod | Main frontal interview shot |
| **Camera B** | 50 mm lens | Tripod | Secondary interview angle |
| **Camera C** | 85 mm lens | Handheld | Close details and additional coverage |

### Camera A: main frontal view

- Position the camera near eye level.
- Use it as the primary visual anchor for the edited sequence.
- Set the lens to **Manual Focus**.
- Mount the **RØDE VideoMic NTG** on top of the camera using the microphone mount adapter, then connect it to the camera’s external microphone input. 
- In the camera’s audio settings, select the external microphone to receive audio.

### Camera B: secondary angle

- Position the camera approximately **30–45 degrees** from the interviewee.
- Use the angle to emphasize depth and facial structure.
- Set the lens to **Manual Focus**.
- Connect the **RØDE Wireless GO II receiver** to the camera.
- Turn on one Wireless GO II **transmitter** and attach the lapel microphone near the interviewee’s collar or upper chest.
- The microphone may be hidden beneath clothing, but fabric must not rub against or obstruct it.
- In the camera’s audio settings, select the external microphone to receive audio.

### Camera C: handheld detail camera

- Record close details such as hands, gestures, clothing, or partial facial features.
- Use restrained handheld movement to add visual variation.
- Set the lens to **Manual Focus**.
- Treat the camera’s audio as reference sound rather than the primary interview recording.

## Exposure Settings

Set the following controls in this order:

1. **White balance:** Controls how the camera interprets the colour of the light.
2. **Shutter speed:** Controls how movement appears and how long each frame is exposed.
3. **Aperture:** Controls how much light enters the camera and the depth of field.
4. **ISO:** Adjusts image brightness; higher values produce more digital noise.

Each lens has a different available aperture range. Select the closest practical value when the same aperture is not available on every lens.

> Keep the white balance and shutter speed consistent across all three cameras. Keep the aperture settings the same or as similar as possible on Cameras A and B. Camera C may require a different aperture or ISO when its framing, subject detail, or position within the lighting setup changes.

## Monitor and test the audio

Connect headphones to Cameras A and B to monitor the audio in real time.

Ask the interviewee to speak at the volume they expect to use during the interview. Listen for:

- Clear, intelligible speech
- Clothing rubbing against the microphone
- Electrical interference
- Background noise
- Distortion or clipping
- Audio that is too quiet
- Audio being recorded by the camera’s internal microphone instead of the external microphone

Aim for strong, consistent levels that remain below clipping. Speech should peak at approximately `-12 dB` to provide sufficient headroom. The audio level must not reach `0 dB`.

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

## Recording time

Each pair will record **two complete takes**.

- Organize a clear recording order within the station group.
- Each pair has approximately **12 minutes**.
- The station group is responsible for managing the schedule.

## Before each take

Each pair may adjust:

- The framing of Camera A
- The position and framing of Camera B
- The framing and intended movement of Camera C
- The intensity and colour temperature of the lights
- The aperture and ISO on Cameras A and B, when required
- The aperture and ISO on Camera C to suit its framing and the illuminated area of the body

Keep the following consistent:

- **Shutter speed:** `1/60` on all cameras
- **Custom White Balance:** Do not change it after the lighting setup has been established
- **Light positions:** Do not move the stands after the station setup has been finalized

> Make exposure changes before recording usable footage. Avoid changing aperture or ISO while the camera is recording a shot that may appear in the final edit.

## Record and synchronize the footage

1. Press **Record** on all three cameras.
2. Stand where all three cameras can see you and perform one loud hand clap.
3. Confirm that the clap is visible and audible on the recordings.
4. Wait approximately **2–3 seconds**.
5. Ask the interviewee to begin speaking.
6. Do not stop any camera before the end of the take.
7. Record the full interview as one continuous clip on all three cameras.
8. Repeat the process for the second take.

The clap creates a shared visual and audio reference that can be used to synchronize the footage during editing.

- Fade in or fade out

- No colour correction in Premiere Pro
- No visual effects or filters
- No animated motion effects
