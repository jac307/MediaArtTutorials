# Rough Cut Screening and Sound Design Production

## Individual project

## Goal

Use the rough-cut screening to identify the revisions needed before picture lock, then gather and prepare the sound materials required for the final sound design.

This week includes two connected stages:

1. **Rough-cut screening and critique**
2. **Sound-design production**

> Attendance, participation, and active engagement during the screening and critique are part of the project rubric.

## Project overview

- **Project type:** Individual
- **In-class activity:** Rough-cut screening and critique
- **Sound-production goal:** Gather and prepare the source materials for the final sound design
- **Revision goal:** Confirm picture lock before Week 12
- **Required audio format:** WAV
- **Audio standard:** 48 kHz / 24-bit
- **Music:** Instrumental only

## Project stages

Complete the following stages in order:

Each student will screen their rough cut in a small critique group.

## Critique focus

Respond to elements already present in the rough cut:

- **Sequence clarity:** Is the story understandable?
- **Editing rhythm:** Do the cuts and shot durations feel intentional?
- **Shot quality:** Are focus, framing, and camera stability usable?
- **Lighting consistency:** Do connected shots appear visually coherent?
- **Continuity:** Do actions, objects, body positions, and directions match across cuts?
- **Basic sound continuity:** Does room tone or production sound support the sequence?

Detailed sound design and creative colour grading are not the focus of this screening.

## Participation expectations

During the critique:

- Watch each film attentively.
- Write specific observations.
- Speak during the discussion.
- Identify strengths as well as problems.
- Explain how a revision might improve the work.
- Respond respectfully.
- Avoid rewriting the film according to your own personal preferences.

Useful feedback identifies what the viewer experienced.

For example:

> The action became difficult to follow when the edit moved from the wide shot to the close-up because the object changed position.

Less useful feedback relies only on preference.

For example:

> I would use a different shot.

## Record the feedback

After your screening, collect:

- Peer feedback in concise bullet points
- One or two comments from the instructor or TA
- Your own observations from watching the work with an audience

Do not assume that every suggestion must be followed. Compare the comments and identify repeated concerns.

## Prioritize the revisions

Create a short revision list organized by priority:

### 1. Essential revisions

Problems that prevent the film from being understood or completed:

- Missing action
- Confusing shot order
- Severe continuity error
- Unusable focus or exposure
- Missing ending
- Excessive duration

### 2. Important revisions

Problems that weaken the film but do not prevent comprehension:

- Uneven pacing
- Repetition
- Weak shot choice
- Visible brightness or white-balance jumps
- Abrupt action transitions

### 3. Optional refinements

Small improvements that can be completed after the essential work:

- Minor timing changes
- Small framing adjustments
- Subtle technical corrections

## Re-recording footage

You may re-record footage when a shot cannot be repaired through editing.

When re-recording:

- Match the location, lighting, wardrobe, props, and performance.
- Use the same resolution and frame rate.
- Match the camera angle and focal length.
- Record complete actions with handles.
- Record matching production sound and room tone.
- Rename and back up the new files using the existing protocol.

## Confirm picture lock

Picture lock means that the:

- Shot order is final.
- Shot duration is final.
- Sequence duration is final.
- Aspect ratio is final.
- No additional video clips will be inserted or removed.

Complete picture lock before beginning the detailed sound design and final colour grading in Week 12.

> No additional submission is required for the visual revisions this week. Bring the updated Premiere Pro project and organized project folder to Week 12.

## Use multiple sound layers

| Audio type | Function |
|---|---|
| **ProdAudio** | Production sound recorded during filming |
| **Ambience** | Background sound that establishes a location or acoustic environment |
| **Foley** | Custom-recorded sound synchronized with visible physical actions |
| **SFX** | Sourced or designed sound effects used to reinforce specific moments |
| **Music** | Instrumental music used as a supporting emotional layer |

> Music must not replace visual storytelling. A strong sound design should combine environmental and action-based sounds rather than relying only on music.

## Build a sound cue list

Create a planning document or table.

Suggested structure:

| Timecode | Visible action or environment | Audio type | Sound needed | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `00:00:03` | Character enters the hallway | Ambience | Interior hallway tone | Record | Needed |
| `00:00:08` | Hand places keys on table | Foley | Keys contacting wood | Record | Needed |
| `00:00:15` | Mechanical device activates | SFX | Low mechanical start | Source | Needed |

Identify:

- Existing production sound that can be reused
- Missing ambience
- Missing Foley
- Sound effects that cannot be recorded safely or realistically
- Music, when appropriate
- Sounds that should remain quiet
- Sounds that should become prominent
- Changes between interior and exterior spaces
- Moments of deliberate silence

## Foley Examples

Foley is custom-recorded sound created to match visible physical actions.

| Visual action | Possible Foley recording |
|---|---|
| Walking | Footsteps on wood, concrete, gravel, or grass |
| Object movement | Contact, dragging, tapping, or placement sounds |
| Clothing movement | Fabric, sleeves, jackets, or bags |
| Opening a bag | Zipper, buckle, fabric, or object movement |
| Door interaction | Handle, hinge, latch, opening, and closing |

## Ambience Examples

Ambience establishes the location and acoustic environment.

- Room tone
- Wind
- Distant traffic
- Public activity
- Ventilation
- Interior mechanical sound
- Birds or natural background sound
- Subtle environmental textures

## Preserve the original recordings

Copy and back up the original files before trimming or processing them.

Do not overwrite the only copy of a recording.

## Licensing requirement

Audio does not need to be in the public domain, but it must be:

- Free to use for the project
- Available under a licence that permits the intended use
- Credited according to the licence requirements

Acceptable licences may include:

- Creative Commons Zero (`CC0`)
- Creative Commons Attribution (`CC BY`)
- Other licences that clearly permit reuse with the required credit

Do not use a file when:

- The licence is unclear.
- The creator prohibits reuse.
- The licence conflicts with the project.
- The file was copied from a commercial film, television program, game, album, or streaming service.
- You cannot identify the creator or source.

## Source SFX

SFX may include:

- Mechanical sounds
- Environmental textures
- Enhanced impacts
- Alarms
- Electronic sounds
- Sounds that are unsafe or impossible to record

Download WAV files whenever available.

## Select music

Music is optional.

When using music:

- Select instrumental music only.
- Do not use lyrics.
- Use music as a supporting layer.
- Do not use music to explain the story.
- Do not allow music to replace ambience, Foley, and action sounds.
- Confirm that the licence permits use in the project.

## Record the source information immediately

For every sourced file, record:

- Sound or music title
- Creator or username
- Website
- Direct source link
- Licence
- Date accessed
- Any attribution wording required by the creator

Do not wait until the final week to reconstruct the credits.

## Prepare the individual sound files

Use **any audio-editing software** to review, trim, and clean each sound file individually.

Do **not** synchronize, layer, or mix the complete soundtrack yet. The final sound design will be completed after picture lock in Week 12.

For each sound:

1. Listen to the complete original recording.
2. Select the strongest take.
3. Trim unnecessary material from the beginning and end.
4. Keep a short amount of sound before and after the intended action.
5. Remove or reduce distracting noise when necessary.
6. Adjust the volume only when the recording is clearly too quiet or too loud.
7. Avoid creative effects, reverberation, heavy compression, or other processing that changes the character of the original sound.
8. Export the prepared file as **WAV at 48 kHz**.
9. Open the exported file and confirm that it plays correctly.
10. Preserve an unchanged copy of the original recording.

## Name the files

Use:

```text
ProjectName_AudioType_SimpleDescription_Take#.wav
```

Audio types:

- `ProdAudio`
- `Ambience`
- `Foley`
- `SFX`
- `Music`

Examples:

```text
Echo_ProdAudio_Door_T01.wav
Echo_Ambience_Hallway_T01.wav
Echo_Foley_Footsteps_T02.wav
Echo_SFX_Alarm_T01.wav
Echo_Music_NightTheme_T01.wav
```

Use short, concrete descriptions.

## Create the Audio Credits PDF

Create:

```text
ProjectName_AudioCredits.pdf
```

Include a table with:

| Filename | Audio type | Title or description | Creator | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For original recordings, identify the student who recorded the sound.

Example:

| Filename | Audio type | Title or description | Creator | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Echo_Foley_Footsteps_T01.wav` | Foley | Footsteps on concrete | Student Name | Original recording | Original work |
| `Echo_SFX_Alarm_T01.wav` | SFX | Mechanical alarm | Username | Freesound | CC BY 4.0 |

Include the direct source URL and access date for every downloaded file.

## Build the folder structure

Create:

```text
Lastname_Firstname_SoundDesignProduction
└── 02_Audio
    ├── 00_Credits
    ├── 01_ProdAudio
    ├── 02_Ambience
    ├── 03_Foley
    ├── 04_SFX
    └── 05_Music
```

Place:

- `ProjectName_AudioCredits.pdf` inside `00_Credits`
- Prepared production audio inside `01_ProdAudio`
- Prepared ambience inside `02_Ambience`
- Prepared Foley inside `03_Foley`
- Prepared sound effects inside `04_SFX`
- Prepared instrumental music inside `05_Music`

Keep the required folder even when one category is not used.

## Compress the package

Compress the complete main folder as:

```text
Lastname_Firstname_SoundDesignProduction.zip
```
Do not compress the subfolders separately.

## Required submission

| Item | Required filename |
