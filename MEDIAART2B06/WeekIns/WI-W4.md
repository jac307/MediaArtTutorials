# Continuous Shot

## Individual project

![Example of a continuous first-person moving shot](imgs/08.png)

## Goal

Create a **30-second continuous handheld video** recorded from a **first-person point of view**, moving from an **outdoor space into an indoor space** (or vice-versa) in one uninterrupted shot.

The central challenge is managing a strong change in exposure while moving between two contrasting lighting environments. You will identify the aperture required for the brightest and darkest parts of the route, then adjust the aperture gradually while recording. Any remaining exposure or colour imbalance will be addressed through **advanced colour correction in Premiere Pro**.

> Attendance, participation, and active engagement during class activities are part of the project rubric.

## Project overview

- **Format:** 30-second continuous video
- **Collaboration:** Work in pairs and submit individually
- **Camera support:** Handheld; no tripod
- **Point of view:** First-person
- **Route:** Outdoor to indoor, or vice-versa
- **Location:** McMaster University campus and immediate surroundings
- **Image orientation:** Landscape

## Required camera settings

- **Camera mode:** Video
- **Shooting mode:** Manual (`M`)
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9
- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps
- **Shutter speed:** `1/60`
- **ISO:** Auto
- **Aperture:** Adjusted manually during the shot
- **Focus:** Auto Focus with the active focus point near the centre of the frame
- **Image Stabilization:** On
- **White balance:** Daylight, Cloudy, or Custom White Balance
- **Metering:** Evaluative or Matrix Metering
- **Histogram:** Visible during rehearsals and recording
- **Audio:** Camera’s built-in microphone

    <span class="section-title">3. Plan the route and exposure transition</span>
    <span class="section-description">

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

Your partner and you may use the same general location, but each student must develop and record an individual approach.

Select a route that moves from an **outdoor space into an indoor space (or vice-versa)** at a normal walking pace.

The route should take approximately **40–60 seconds** to complete so you can trim the beginning or end to create a final 30-second video.

## Route requirements

- Include a clear transition from outdoor to indoor light, or from indoor to outdoor light.
- Use a door, entrance, corridor, or similar spatial threshold.
- Avoid stairs.
- Avoid running or abrupt movement.
- Do not block doors, walkways, roads, or accessibility routes.
- Confirm that another student can safely assist with opening doors when necessary.
- Select a route that presents a meaningful difference between the brightest and darkest areas.
- Consider the weather and available daylight on the day of recording when selecting the route.
- Choose an indoor space that creates an exposure challenge without being almost completely dark in comparison with the exterior.

> The route must create a clear exposure challenge. A route that remains under similar lighting conditions does not address the main technical objective of Week 4. However, the contrast should remain manageable. Avoid combining a very bright exterior with an almost completely dark interior, as this may produce a difference that cannot be controlled effectively during recording or corrected successfully in post-production.

## Develop your individual approach

Although equipment and general locations may be shared, decide individually:

- Where the camera will look
- What remains near the centre of the frame
- How the camera will move through the space
- Which visual details will receive attention
- How the doorway or transition point will be framed
- How the camera movement will communicate a first-person perspective
- Where the exposure adjustment will begin
- How quickly the aperture will change

> Your framing, movement, timing, and interpretation of the route should be distinct from those of the other students in your group.

    <span class="section-description">

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

Each student should record **two-three takes**.

## Recording rules

- Do not stop or restart the camera during a take.
- Do not make cuts during recording.
- Do not run.
- Walk at a normal, controlled pace.
- Do not cover the camera’s built-in microphone.
- Avoid unnecessary contact with the microphone area.
- Keep the main point of interest near the active centre focus point.
- Monitor the histogram while approaching and crossing the lighting transition.
- Open the aperture gradually as you enter the darker interior.
- Continue recording for several seconds after reaching the final indoor position.

Classmates may assist by:

- Opening doors
- Watching for obstacles
- Temporarily clearing the path
- Monitoring equipment safety

> Assistance must remain secondary. Each student is responsible for operating the camera, framing the shot, monitoring the histogram, and adjusting the aperture.

## Between takes

After reviewing the first take, you may change:

- The starting position
- The final position
- The route
- The camera angle
- The walking pace
- The outdoor aperture
- The indoor aperture
- The timing of the aperture adjustment
- The selected fixed White Balance

Complete all changes before beginning the second take.

    <span class="section-title">7. Colour-correct and export Version 1</span>
    <span class="section-description">
      Edit the continuous shot in Premiere Pro, correct the changing exposure, balance the natural sound, and export the technical version.
    </span>
  </summary>

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

Edit the video and sound in **Adobe Premiere Pro**.

Before beginning, review the [W4 Tutorials](../Tutorials/index.html?file=T-W4.json){:target="_blank"} and relevant tutorials from previous weeks.

Pay particular attention to:

- Importing and organizing footage
- Lumetri Scopes
- Lumetri Color
- Exposure and colour keyframes
- Audio levels
- Titles and credits
- H.264 exporting

## Create the sequence

- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps
- **Final duration:** Exactly 30 seconds

## Edit the continuous shot

- Select one complete take.
- Trim only the beginning or end.
- Do not make cuts within the shot.
- Do not change the playback speed.
- Add only a simple fade-in at the beginning and fade-out at the end.
- Use the original camera sound.
- Do not add music.

## Apply advanced colour correction

Use **Lumetri Color**, **Lumetri Scopes**, and **keyframes** to respond to the changing exposure and colour over time.

Your correction should:

- Address the contrast between the indoor and outdoor environments
- Protect important highlight information in the brighter section
- Improve the visibility of important shadow information in the darker section
- Correct exposure and colour gradually as the camera moves
- Maintain natural colour and contrast
- Avoid making the two environments look artificially identical
- Preserve the visible experience of moving between different lighting conditions

Possible adjustments include:

- Exposure
- Contrast
- Highlights
- Shadows
- Whites
- Blacks
- Colour temperature
- Tint

Use keyframes when different parts of the shot require different corrections.

> The purpose of colour correction is not to erase the lighting transition. Preserve the spatial and atmospheric difference between the two environments while improving the continuity and legibility of the image.

## Edit the natural sound

Use only the original sound recorded by the camera.

- Listen to the complete recording using headphones.
- Adjust the audio level so the natural sound remains clear and audible.
- Use audio keyframes or fades when levels change abruptly.
- Reduce unwanted noise when possible.
- Check the Premiere Pro audio meters throughout the sequence.
- Keep the final audio below clipping.
- The master audio level must not reach <code>0 dB</code>.

## Titles and credits

Add the title and credits during the final part of the video.

Include:

- Project title
- Your name
- Year

The text must be:

- Readable
- Spatially considered
- Visible long enough to be read
- Placed over the continuous video image

## Version 1 restrictions

- No cuts within the shot
- No speed changes
- No music
- No expressive colour grading
- No additional footage
- No stock footage or stock audio
- No visual effects that obscure the original image
- Maintain a natural visual appearance

## Export Version 1

- **Format:** MP4
- **Codec:** H.264
- **Resolution:** 1920 × 1080 pixels
- **Filename:** `Lastname-Name-ContinuousShot-ColourCorrection.mp4`

> Export and review Version 1 before beginning the Expressive Version. Confirm that the video plays correctly, the exposure correction changes gradually, and the natural sound remains clearly audible.

The second version must use the same visual sequence as Version 1:

- Use the same beginning and ending points.
- Maintain the same 30-second duration.
- Do not add cuts.
- Do not change the playback speed.
- Do not add other footage.

## Define the expressive approach

Before editing, identify the feeling or narrative approach you want to create.

Consider:

- How the movement between spaces may suggest a change in mood
- How the first-person perspective may imply memory, tension, curiosity, isolation, anticipation, or another experience
- How colour can affect the interpretation of the route
- How music can influence pace, atmosphere, and emotional response
- Whether the indoor and outdoor spaces should feel connected or contrasting

> The expressive choices should develop from the original movement and spatial transition rather than appearing as unrelated decoration.

## Apply expressive colour grading

After completing the technical colour correction, apply a colour grade that supports the intended feeling or narrative.

You may adjust:

- Colour temperature
- Tint
- Saturation
- Contrast
- Highlights and shadows
- Whites and blacks
- Individual colour channels
- Curves
- Colour wheels
- Vignette
- Other Lumetri Color controls

Use **keyframes** when the colour grade changes during the movement between spaces.

Colour changes should:

- Develop gradually
- Respond to the camera’s movement
- Support the intended emotional or narrative approach
- Remain intentional throughout the sequence
- Preserve enough image information for the scene to remain readable

> Colour grading may transform the atmosphere of the recording, but it should not obscure the image or make important visual information unreadable.

## Select and add music

You may add **openly licensed or public-domain music**.

Before using the music:

- Confirm that the licence permits reuse.
- Record the music title.
- Record the creator’s name.
- Record the source.
- Record the licence or required attribution.

When editing the music:

- Connect it to the intended feeling or narrative.
- Use volume keyframes and fades.
- Balance it with any natural sound you choose to keep.
- Monitor the mix using headphones and Premiere Pro audio meters.
- Keep the final mix below clipping.
- The master audio level must not reach <code>0 dB</code>.

You may:

- Keep the natural camera sound beneath the music
- Reduce the natural sound
- Use selected moments of natural sound
- Remove the natural sound when this supports the intended approach

## Titles and credits

Add the title and credits during the final part of the video.

Include:

- Project title
- Your name
- Year
- Music title
- Music creator
- Music source
- Licence or attribution information
