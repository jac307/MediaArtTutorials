## Objective  
In this session, you will bring together all parts of your collaborative analysis, ensuring a **cohesive and unified voice**. You will review and merge individual sections (Opening, Body Paragraphs, Closing), refine ideas, and prepare the supporting materials for your final submission. You will also begin brainstorming how to present your archival entry visually for the upcoming mini-presentation session.  

By the end of this session, you will have:  
- Integrated and revised your **Opening, Body, and Closing** into a single unified draft.  
- Ensured the analysis has a consistent tone and unique perspective.  
- Started gathering all archival entry materials for submission.  
- Sketched initial ideas for visually presenting your project as a poster.  

---

## Materials Required
- Draft Opening and Closing sections (from previous class)  
- Individual body paragraph drafts  
- Annotated bibliography  
- Access to collected images, videos, and documentation of selected artworks/projects  
- Shared document platform (Google Docs, Word Online, etc.)  

---

## Activities  
**Complete the following in order. Ask your professor for guidance if needed.**

---

### <span style="color: darkred;">[40 minutes] Combine and Review Draft Sections</span>  

1. **Merge All Sections**  
   - Combine your Opening, both Body Paragraphs, and Closing into a single shared document.  
   - Read the entire draft aloud together to check for flow and consistency.  

2. **Share Insights and Refine**  
   - Discuss any new ideas, connections, or observations since drafting.  
   - Identify places where transitions feel abrupt or voice changes between sections.  

3. **Revise for Cohesion**  
   - Adjust wording to ensure the analysis sounds like it was written by one voice.  
   - Eliminate redundancy and ensure each section directly supports the main argument or perspective.  

---

### <span style="color: darkred;">Analysis Structure & Section Goals</span>  

> Use this structure to guide your revision — every section should respond to specific goals.

**Opening (40–60 words)**  
- Introduce the artist/collective and the focus of your analysis.  
- Name the two selected artworks/projects.  
- Present the central perspective or question that links them.  

**Body Paragraph 1 (150–180 words)**  
- Analyze the first selected artwork/project.  
- Describe its medium, form, and subject.  
- Explain how it engages with **body, identity, and equity**.  
- Integrate insights from your research (APA citations).  
- Connect the work to the artist’s/collective’s broader practice.  

**Body Paragraph 2 (150–180 words)**  
- Follow the same approach as Paragraph 1 for the second artwork/project.  
- Maintain parallel structure for easy comparison between the two analyses.  

**Closing (40–60 words)**  
- Synthesize connections between the two works.  
- Emphasize the relevance, impact, or innovation of the artist’s/collective’s practice.  

---

### <span style="color: darkred;">[20 minutes] Archival Entry Materials</span>  

Begin gathering all required components for your final archival entry:  

- **Artist Bio** – 100 words, including background and focus of their work  
- **Country** – Based on the artist/collective’s base or origin  
- **Artworks Documentation** – For both selected works:  
  - Image or screenshot (high quality)  
  - Title, year, medium/technical specs  
  - 1–2 sentence description  
  - Link to artwork/project (if available)  

---

### <span style="color: darkred;">[30 minutes] Compile Draft for Submission</span>  

In a **single PDF document**, include:  
1. Final unified draft analysis (300–400 words, APA citations)  
2. Artist bio (100 words)  
3. Country  
4. Documentation for 2 artworks/projects (image, details, link)  
5. Annotated bibliography  

📄 **Filename:** `P1-DraftFullEntry-Group-#.pdf`  
> Include both partners’ full names and student numbers at the top.  

---

### <span style="color: darkred;">[20 minutes] Poster Presentation Brainstorm</span>  

As part of the mini-presentation session, you will **present your archival entry as a visually designed poster**.  

- **Goal:** Translate your PDF content into a **clear, engaging, and visually appealing format** for public viewing.  
- In your group, create a quick **sketch or layout plan** for how the information will appear on the poster.  
- Consider:  
  - Which images will be the focus?  
  - How will you balance text and visuals?  
  - What visual style or colour palette reflects your artist’s/collective’s work?  
  - How will you make the thematic connections between artworks clear to viewers?  

💡 *Tip:* Think of your poster as a curated window into your research — it should be informative, but also inviting and visually memorable.
