<h1 style="color: darkred;">W2: Artist/Collective Selection & Initial Research</h1>  
<p><strong>P1: Embodied Archival Entry · Groups of 2 students</strong></p>

## Objective  
Students will begin building the **research foundation** for their *Embodied Archival Entry* project by selecting an artist or collective from the provided list and gathering initial materials.  

By the end of this session, students will have:  
- Chosen their artist/collective.  
- Create an annotated bibliography in **APA format**.
- Begin reading selected resources.   

---

## Materials Required
- Digital or physical notebook for organizing citations and ideas  
- Laptop or device for research  
- Access to McMaster Library databases & Avenue to Learn  

---

## Activities  
**Complete the following in order. Ask your professor for guidance if needed.**

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[10 minutes] Artist/Collective Selection</h3>

With your **partner**, select one artist/collective from the provided list.  
- Review the list quickly together.  
- Choose someone whose work strongly relates to **body, identity, and equity**.  
- Identify **3–4 key artworks** from this artist/collective.  
- Divide the artworks so that each partner will be responsible for researching 1–2 of them in depth.

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[30 minutes] Initial Research & Source Gathering</h3>

Open a shared document (Google Docs or Word Online) and begin collecting:  

#### 1. **Annotated Bibliography – First Step**  
- Find **5–7 potential references** (mix of academic and public sources).  
- At this stage, skim and save — do not read in full yet.  
- Types of sources to look for:  
  - Academic articles, artist interviews, exhibition catalogs, essays, or documentaries.  
  - Public-facing media: artist talks, news coverage, reputable blogs, or museum websites.  
- Ask:  
  - *What is the artist’s/collective’s background?*  
  - *What influences shaped their work?*  
  - *What styles, techniques, and themes are prevalent?*  
  - *Are there interviews or statements where they discuss their work?*

> **Recommendation:** Search in both English and other languages you speak. Go beyond the first page of Google results. Use library databases for scholarly material.

---

#### 2. **Multimedia Materials**  
- Find **5–7 multimedia items** related to your artist/collective.  
- These can include:  
  - Images of artworks  
  - Video clips or recordings  
  - Audio works or interviews  
  - Links to online exhibitions or archives  
- Record basic details: title, year, medium, and source link.

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[20 minutes] Sharing & Refining</h3>
  
- Share your findings with your partner.  
- Remove duplicate sources and select the **strongest 3–4** references for now.  
- Compare multimedia materials and keep the most relevant examples.  
- Make notes on what’s missing — list gaps to follow up on before next week.

---

## Before the Next Class
1. Expand your **annotated bibliography** to **10–12 total resources** (shared between both partners).  
   - Format all entries in **APA style**.  
   - For each, write a **3–5 sentence abstract** summarizing the source’s main points and its relevance to your project.  
2. Finalize your **multimedia list** to include at least **5 strong items**.  
3. Keep a running list of themes, techniques, and contextual details for your artist/collective.
