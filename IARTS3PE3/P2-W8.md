<h1 style="color: darkred;">P2 - In-class Work 2</h1>  
<p><strong>P2: Research–Creation Project · Groups of 3–4 students</strong></p>

## Objective  
In this session, you will build on your research and brainstorming from previous sessions to define the **type and scope** of your artistic response project. You will ensure the project is achievable within a **3-week timeframe** while remaining conceptually grounded in the work of your selected artist(s)/collective(s).  

By the end of this session, you will have:  
- Reviewed both your **individual research** and **group brainstorming** notes.  
- Selected a **final project direction** (performance, music, visual art, digital art, installation, hybrid, etc.).  
- Defined a clear conceptual link between your project and the chosen artist(s)/collective(s).  
- Outlined the project steps and gathered initial materials needed for production.  

---

## Materials Required
- Your **Group P2 Notes** and **Individual annotated resources / research notes** from [P2 - In-class Work 1](P2-W7.md)  
- Laptop or device for writing and documentation  
- Access to the selected artist(s)/collective(s) works (images, videos, sound, etc.)  
- Digital or physical sketching/drafting tools  

---

## Activities  
**Complete the following in order. Ask your professor for guidance if needed.**

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[20 minutes] Review & Connect</h3>

1. **Review Previous Work**  
   - As a group, revisit your notes from P2 – In-class Work 1.  
   - Share new resources, observations, or insights found individually.  

2. **Identify Common Ground**  
   - Discuss the **core ideas** and **methods** that connect the selected artist(s)/collective(s) to your own voices and lived experiences.  
   - Make sure your response will highlight **identity** through **community**, **body**, and/or **place**.  
   - Ask yourselves:  
     - What thematic link ties our work to the artists’?  
     - Which techniques, materials, or approaches can we adapt?  
     - How can we make this *our own* while maintaining a clear dialogue with their work?  

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[30 minutes] Project Definition</h3>

1. **Select Project Type**  
   - Performance, music/sound art, visual art, digital art, installation, hybrid, etc.  
   - Consider feasibility within **3 weeks** (time, skills, and available resources).  

2. **Write a Project Description** (150–200 words) in your shared document under **"Project Definition"**:  
   - Working title (optional)  
   - Project type and format  
   - Main concept and thematic focus  
   - Specific references to selected artist(s)/collective(s)  
   - How it reflects your group’s identity and perspective  
   - The thematic connection (community, body, and/or place)  

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[40 minutes] Drafting & Materials Gathering</h3>

1. **Create Detailed Drafts**  
   - Sketch, storyboard, script, or outline your project in detail.  
   - Assign roles and responsibilities to each member.  

2. **Make a Materials List**  
   - List all materials, tools, software, or equipment needed.  
   - Indicate what you already have and what needs to be acquired or booked.  

3. **Gather Initial Materials**  
   - Collect reference images, sound samples, textures, colour palettes, or any multimedia elements that will shape the work.  

➡️ Save your work as a **PDF**  
📄 **Filename:** `Group-#-P2-DefinitionDrafts.pdf`  
> Include **full names** and **student numbers** of all group members at the top.  

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">Before the Next Class</h3>

- Ensure your group has a **complete draft** of your concept and the majority of your materials ready for the first iteration.  
- Individually, continue gathering inspiration and refining elements you are responsible for.
