<p><strong>P1: Embodied Archival Entry · Groups of 2 students</strong></p>

## Objective  
Students will build on their individual research by sharing insights from the sources they read, identifying thematic and conceptual connections, and drafting a **collaborative 300–400 word analysis** of their chosen artist/collective. This draft will form the core of their curatorial text.

By the end of this session, students will have:  
- Shared key takeaways from their research.  
- Selected **two artworks/projects** to reference and analyze further.  
- Written a 300–400 word **draft analysis** in a shared document.

---

## Materials Required
- Digital or physical notebook for brainstorming ideas  
- Laptop or device for writing  
- Access to your annotated bibliography and collected multimedia materials  
- Shared document platform (Google Docs, Word Online, etc.)  

---

## Activities  
**Complete the following in order. Ask your professor for guidance as needed.**

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[30 minutes] Activity 1: Group Brainstorming</h3>

1. **Share Insights from Research**  
   - Each partner takes 5–7 minutes to present the most relevant information from their annotated bibliography and multimedia materials.  
   - Highlight:  
     - Key themes, concepts, or methods found in your sources  
     - Notable quotes or ideas from interviews, essays, or reviews  
     - Any contextual information (historical, cultural, political) that deepens understanding of the artist/collective’s work

2. **Open a Shared Document** and create a section titled **"Brainstorming Notes"**.  
   - Use bullet points to capture key ideas from both partners.  
   - Answer the following guiding questions to help decide the focus of your collaborative analysis:  
     - What recurring themes or concepts appear in the artist/collective’s work?  
     - How does their work address **body, identity, and equity**?  
     - Which two artworks/projects best represent these themes?  
     - What connections can be drawn between the artworks and the artist’s/collective’s broader practice?  
     - How can we link these ideas to the course discussions so far?

3. **Select Two Artworks/Projects**  
   - Decide which two artworks will be referenced and analyzed in depth.  
   - Note their titles, years, mediums, and any relevant contextual details.  
   - Gather the best images, videos, or documentation to reference during writing.

---

<h3 style="color: darkred;">[50 minutes] Activity 2: Collaborative Draft Analysis</h3>

**New:** The draft analysis should be **300–400 words total**.  
- Each partner writes **150–180 words** to allow space for bridging both sections into a unified draft.  
- Maintain a shared document so both can read and adjust each other’s work as it develops.  
- Agree on **tone, vocabulary, and perspective** before starting to ensure a consistent voice.

### Recommended Structure
1. **Opening (40–60 words)**  
   - Briefly introduce the artist/collective and the overall focus of your analysis.  
   - Mention the two selected artworks.

2. **Body Paragraphs (200–250 words)**  
   - Each partner writes one paragraph analyzing one of the selected artworks.  
   - Include:  
     - Description of the work (medium, form, subject)  
     - Analysis of how it engages with body, identity, and equity  
     - Insights from your research sources (cite as needed in APA style)  
   - Connect the artwork to the artist’s/collective’s broader practice.

3. **Closing (40–60 words)**  
   - Bring the two analyses together by identifying thematic or conceptual connections.  
   - End with a statement on the relevance or impact of the artist/collective’s work.
