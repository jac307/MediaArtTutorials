# P1: Critical Research-Creation Profile

## Version 2: Critical Connections · Pairs

**Goal:** Develop the first written version of your profile. Revise the research direction from Version 1, confirm the selected artworks, connect your findings to course readings and other sources, and prepare the image files needed for the next version.

> This is not the final blog entry. The writing, artwork selection, thesis, and images may change after further research and revision.

## What you will complete

- A short record of changes made since Version 1
- An artist or collective profile
- A revised research question and working thesis
- Written entries for three artworks or projects
- A critical connections section using course readings and other sources
- An annotated bibliography with six core sources
- A folder of high-quality images
- An updated division-of-work table
- A one-slide rapid presentation

## Division of labour

Both partners are responsible for the full document and ZIP package. Divide the initial work as follows:

| Partner A | Partner B |
|---|---|
| Read and annotate one course reading | Read and annotate one course reading |
| Read and annotate one additional academic source | Read and annotate one additional academic source |
| Annotate one primary artist or critical/contextual source | Annotate one primary artist or critical/contextual source |
| Take the lead on one artwork entry | Take the lead on one artwork entry |
| Gather and credit images for assigned material | Gather and credit images for assigned material |

Review the third artwork and write the critical connections section together. Both partners must read the complete document, check all sources and image credits, and understand the full argument before submitting.

> Do not divide the project into two isolated halves. Individual sections should be revised together so the document uses a consistent focus, voice, and citation style.

## Record changes since Version 1

Write **100–150 words** explaining how the project changed after further research and discussion.

Address:

- What did you narrow, remove, or add?
- Did the main research question change?
- Did any source or artwork become more important?
- What did you learn that changed your understanding of the artist?

Be specific. Do not state only that you “did more research” or “made improvements.”

## Write the artist or collective profile

Write **150–200 words** introducing:

- The artist or collective
- Location and relevant cultural, social, political, or historical context
- Main media, materials, technologies, or methods
- Central themes or questions in the practice
- Relevant collaborations, communities, or audiences

Use the artist's preferred name, pronouns, and self-description when available. Distinguish information stated by the artist from interpretations written by other people.

Include one high-quality profile image of the artist or collective. If a profile image is unavailable or inappropriate, use an authorized image that represents the collective or practice.

The profile image must include:

- A caption
- Photographer or creator credit, when available
- Source link
- Alt text

## Revise the research questions

Include:

- One revised main research question
- Two or three supporting questions

The questions should connect with the confirmed artworks and sources. Remove questions that are too broad, factual, or unrelated to the direction the research has taken.

## Write a working thesis

Write **75–125 words** stating what you currently understand or argue about the artist's practice.

The working thesis should:

- Answer the main research question
- Identify the main focus of the profile
- Name the artistic strategies, methods, or concerns that support your position
- Recognize an important tension, question, or limit when relevant

The thesis is not a list of themes. It should make a clear statement that can be supported through the selected artworks and sources.

> The thesis is provisional. You may revise it in Versions 3 and 4.

## Confirm three artworks or projects

Select three artworks or projects for the profile. Confirm that they provide enough material to address the thesis and research questions.

For each work, verify:

- Complete title and year
- Medium, materials, duration, dimensions, or format
- Exhibition, performance, publication, or project context when relevant
- Reliable source links
- Available images, video, audio, or other documentation

## Write the artwork entries

Create one section for each of the three selected artworks or projects. Write **150–200 words per work**.

Each entry must include:

- Title and year
- Medium, materials, duration, dimensions, or format
- Presentation or production context when relevant
- Description of what the audience sees, hears, encounters, or does
- Analysis of the artist's methods, materials, techniques, or decisions
- Connection to the artist's broader practice
- Connection to the working thesis or research questions
- Research evidence with APA in-text citations

Do not rely only on description. Explain how specific artistic choices produce meaning or shape the audience's experience.

Include at least one image or other form of documentation for each work. Every item must have a caption, credit, source link, and alt text.

For time-based, interactive, or performance work, include a stable link and relevant timestamps when possible. Do not include complete downloaded video or audio files in the ZIP package.

## Write the critical connections

Write **500–700 words** connecting the three artworks to:

- Both selected course readings
- At least two additional academic, primary artist, or critical/contextual sources
- Relevant EDI and anti-oppression concepts

Organize the section around **two or three critical connections**. Possible areas include:

- Testimony and representation
- Positionality and accountability
- Care and consent
- Visibility and refusal
- Disability justice and access
- Technology and systemic power
- Land, migration, or cultural memory
- Community-building and collaboration
- Queer or trans-feminist worldbuilding

For each connection:

1. Explain the concept in your own words.
2. Use a passage or argument from one course reading.
3. Explain how the second reading supports, extends, or challenges it.
4. Connect the concept to specific evidence from one or more artworks.
5. Use another source to add context or a different interpretation.
6. Explain what the artwork helps you understand, question, or reconsider.

Do not write separate summaries of Reading 1, Reading 2, and the artist. Bring them together around shared questions, differences, or tensions.

Use APA in-text citations and include page numbers for quotations and specific passages. Use timestamps for relevant sections of audio or video sources.

## Prepare the annotated bibliography

Select six core sources from the Version 1 source map:

| Source type | Required |
|---|---:|
| Course readings | 2 |
| Additional academic sources | 2 |
| Primary artist source | 1 |
| Critical or contextual source | 1 |

Write **75–100 words per annotation**. Each annotation must:

- Identify the source's main argument or useful information
- Note one concept, passage, example, or detail relevant to the project
- Explain how the source was used or will be used
- Identify an important limit, bias, or missing perspective when relevant
- Include useful page numbers or timestamps

Use this structure:

| APA reference and link | Source type | Annotation |
|---|---|---|
| Complete reference | Course, academic, primary artist, or critical/contextual | 75–100 words addressing the required points. |

Include a complete APA reference list for every source cited in the document. Do not include sources in the reference list if they are not cited. You may keep unused sources in your private research notes for later versions.

## Research and writing ethics

- Use evidence for claims about the artist, artworks, communities, and contexts.
- Do not assume the artist's identity, intentions, beliefs, or community relationships.
- Do not reduce the artist or community to trauma, discrimination, or identity categories.
- Clearly distinguish the artist's statements from your interpretation and from other writers' arguments.
- Provide enough context for sensitive images, language, or subject matter.
- Do not copy source descriptions, gallery text, captions, or artist statements without quotation marks and citations.
- Write source notes and analysis in your own words.

## Prepare the main document

Export one PDF containing:

1. Both partners' full names and student numbers
2. Changes since Version 1
3. Artist or collective profile and profile image
4. Revised main and supporting research questions
5. Working thesis
6. Three artwork entries with embedded images or documentation
7. Critical connections section
8. Annotated bibliography
9. Updated division-of-work table
10. Complete APA reference list

The PDF must include working links. Check that all text, images, captions, credits, and tables are readable before exporting.

Name the document:

`P1_Critical-Connections.pdf`

## Prepare the image folder

Create a folder named `Images` containing:

- One profile image of the artist or collective
- At least one image for each selected artwork or project
- Additional images used in the document, when relevant

Use `.JPG` or `.PNG` files. Use the highest-quality version available from a reliable source.

Image files should:

- Be at least **1600 pixels on the longest side** when a file of that size is available
- Keep their original proportions
- Remain unaltered except for necessary cropping
- Not be enlarged from a small or low-quality file
- Not be screenshots when a downloadable image is available
- Use short, clear filenames without spaces or special characters

Use the following filename structure:

```text
Artist-Profile.jpg
Artwork-01_Short-Title.jpg
Artwork-02_Short-Title.jpg
Artwork-03_Short-Title.jpg
```

If one artwork requires more than one image:

```text
Artwork-01a_Short-Title.jpg
Artwork-01b_Short-Title.jpg
```

## Include image and media information

Add the following table to the main PDF:

| Filename | Artist/artwork | Caption information | Source and credit | Alt text | Public-use notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Image filename | Identify the person, artwork, or project | Title, year, medium, and other available information | Creator, photographer, organization, and link | Brief description of the image | Note any stated licence, permission, or restriction. |

Finding an image online does not mean it can be republished publicly. Record the source and any available rights information now so unsuitable images can be replaced before the profile is posted on the course blog.

## Record and review the division of work

Update the division-of-work table from Version 1:

| Task | Partner responsible | Status or notes |
|---|---|---|
| Example: Draft Artwork Entry 1 | Full name | Drafted and reviewed by both partners |

Before submitting, both partners must:

- Read and revise the full document.
- Check every citation, quotation, image credit, and link.
- Confirm that the working thesis is supported by the selected artworks and sources.
- Check that the writing uses a consistent voice and structure.
- Open every image file and confirm that it matches the filename and caption.
- Confirm that the ZIP package opens and contains the required folders and files.

## Prepare the one-minute presentation slide

During the following lecture, each pair will give a rapid **one-minute presentation**. Presentations will take place one after another, without questions or class feedback.

Prepare one slide in image format containing:

- The artist or collective
- The working thesis in one or two lines
- Images of the three selected artworks or projects
- Two or three central concepts guiding the critical connections

Slide requirements:

- Submit one `.JPG` or `.PNG` image.
- Use a 16:9 format, preferably `1920 × 1080 px`.
- Keep the text brief and readable.
- Include the title, year, and image credit for each artwork.
- Choose one speaker.
- Do not exceed one minute.
- Name the slide `P1_Critical-Connections-Slide.jpg` or `P1_Critical-Connections-Slide.png`.

Slides must be submitted in advance so they can be presented in sequence without changing computers.

## Create the ZIP package

Use the following structure:

```text
P1_Critical-Connections.zip
├── P1_Critical-Connections.pdf
└── Images
    ├── Artist-Profile.jpg
    ├── Artwork-01_Short-Title.jpg
    ├── Artwork-02_Short-Title.jpg
    └── Artwork-03_Short-Title.jpg
```

One partner submits the files on behalf of the pair.
