### P1: Critical Research-Creation Profile

# Version 4: Poster Session

**Work format:** Pairs and individual engagement  
**What to bring:** Printed poster, writing materials, and brief presentation notes  
**Individual submission:** Poster Session Engagement Form

## Goal

Share your research profile and preliminary research-creation direction, learn from the work of other pairs, and identify ideas that may inform Project 2.

The class will be divided into **Group A** and **Group B**. During each one-hour period, one group will present while the other group visits the posters. After a 10-minute break, the groups will exchange roles.

Arrive five minutes before the session begins and remain engaged throughout the complete session. If any part of the poster session creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an alternative way to participate that meets the same learning goals.

## What you must bring

- Your printed 18 × 24-inch vertical research poster
- A pen or pencil for the Poster Session Engagement Form
- Any brief notes you need to discuss your research and research-creation direction

> An electronic or large-print version of the engagement form can be provided when needed. Contact the instructor before the session so it is ready for you.

#### When your pair is presenting

Both partners must remain with the poster and contribute to the conversation. You may divide the speaking in a way that works for your pair.

Be prepared to explain:

- why you selected the artist or collective;
- the main research question and working thesis;
- the two critical connections guiding the profile;
- how the selected artworks support or complicate your argument;
- the course readings and other sources that shaped your interpretation;
- your preliminary research-creation proposal; and
- the artistic strategy, medium, technology, or question you may explore further.

This is a conversation, not a memorized formal presentation. You may use brief notes. Begin with a short explanation, respond to questions, and invite visitors to discuss the artist’s work and possible research-creation directions.

Do not read all the poster text aloud. Use its images and sections to guide the conversation and provide additional information or clarification.

#### When you are visiting

Move through the room rather than remaining with one pair or speaking only with people you already know.

During your visiting period:

- visit the posters presented by the other group;
- speak with the presenting pairs and ask relevant questions;
- listen to each explanation before completing your notes;
- discuss the artist or collective, research findings, and possible research-creation directions;
- complete your Poster Session Engagement Form individually; and
- respect the space needed by other visitors and move to another poster when a conversation has finished.

Presenters must remain available to visitors, and visitors must continue circulating and engaging throughout the assigned period.

#### Discussion prompts

You may use these questions to begin or extend a conversation:

- What interested your pair most about this artist or collective?
- Which connection between the readings and artworks became most important?
- Did the research change how you understood either artwork?
- What tension or unresolved question remains in your analysis?
- Which part of the artist’s approach influenced the research-creation proposal?
- What new medium, technology, method, or skill might the proposal require?
- What ethical questions would need to be considered if the idea were developed?

#### Complete the Poster Session Engagement Form

Each student must complete their own handout during the session. The handout is an individual record of your participation and must be written in your own words.

#### Part 1: Poster engagement

Complete entries for **four posters presented by different pairs**.

For each poster:

- record the artist or collective’s name;
- identify one or two specific ideas you learned; and
- identify a theme, medium, strategy, technology, or approach that stood out.

You are still expected to engage with the presenting pairs throughout your visiting period, even though you will write about four posters.

#### Part 2: Connection to Project 2

Identify the artist or collective that stood out most to you. Briefly explain:

- why the artist or collective interested you; and
- how you might extend the pair’s research-creation proposal or make the idea distinct through a different theme, medium, technology, method, audience, or context.

This response is preliminary and does not commit you to a final Project 2 direction.

#### Review and submit the form

Before submitting, confirm that:

- [ ] I included my full name.
- [ ] I completed entries for four posters presented by different pairs.
- [ ] I recorded specific ideas rather than general comments.
- [ ] I completed the connection to Project 2.
- [ ] I wrote the responses in my own words.

Submit the completed form at the end of class.

## Participation expectations

| Requirement | Expectation |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Arrive five minutes before the session begins. |
| Printed poster | Bring the completed 18 × 24-inch poster. |
| Setup | Install and remove the poster using the provided materials at the assigned time. |
