# P1: Critical Research-Creation Profile

## Version 3: Multimodal Profile Draft

**Work format:** Pairs  
**Submission:** Three accessible PDFs per pair and one printed poster  
**Poster format:** 18 × 24 inches, vertical

## Goal

Develop the first complete draft of your multimodal profile. Refine the materials from Version 2, write the critical analysis, develop a preliminary research-creation idea, and design a research poster for the poster session.

This is not the final public profile. The writing, thesis, research-creation proposal, images, and poster may change after the poster session and further revision.

## What you will complete

- A 500–700-word critical analysis
- A revised annotated bibliography with eight core sources
- A 150–200-word preliminary research-creation proposal
- An annotated visual development page
- A digitally designed and printed 18 × 24-inch research poster
- A text alternative for the poster’s content and images

## How to divide the work

Both partners are responsible for the critical analysis, research-creation proposal, poster, and final files. Divide the initial work as follows:

| Partner A | Partner B |
|---|---|
| Lead the first critical connection | Lead the second critical connection |
| Develop selected parts of the research-creation concept | Develop selected parts of the research-creation concept |
| Prepare selected parts of the visual development page | Prepare selected parts of the visual development page |
| Prepare selected poster content | Prepare selected poster content |
| Complete assigned poster design and production tasks | Complete assigned poster design and production tasks |

Do not divide the new materials into two separate halves. Revise every component together so the submission has a consistent argument, voice, and visual approach.

#### Review the Version 2 outline

Before writing, review:

- the main research question and supporting questions;
- the working thesis;
- the two critical connections;
- the evidence selected from both artworks; and
- the passages, concepts, and examples selected from the eight core sources.

Refine these materials before turning the outline into complete paragraphs.

#### Write the critical analysis

Develop the Version 2 outline into a **500–700-word critical analysis**.

The analysis must connect:

- both selected artworks or projects;
- both course readings;
- at least two additional academic sources;
- at least one primary artist source; and
- at least one critical or contextual source.

Use the following structure.

##### Introduction

Establish the main research question and focus of the analysis. Present the working thesis as your answer to the question. Identify the two concepts and artworks that will organize the discussion.

##### First critical connection and Artwork 1

Develop the first part of the thesis by explaining one critical concept. Bring both course readings into conversation through a point of agreement, difference, or tension. Analyze specific evidence from the first artwork and use another source to add context or a different interpretation.

##### Second critical connection and Artwork 2

Develop the second part of the thesis through another critical concept and the second artwork. Explain how this connection differs from, extends, or complicates the argument developed in the previous section.

##### Conclusion

Bring the two critical connections together and explain how they answer the main research question. Identify what the artworks help you understand or reconsider. Acknowledge an important tension, limitation, or unresolved question.

#### Follow the writing requirements

- Bring readings, sources, and artworks into conversation instead of summarizing them separately.
- Use specific evidence from both artworks.
- Write in your own words.
- Use APA in-text citations.
- Separate the artist’s statements, other writers’ arguments, and your own interpretation.
- Revise the full analysis together so it reads as one text.

#### Prepare the writing PDF

Submit one PDF per pair containing:

1. Both partners’ full names and student numbers
2. Artist or collective name
3. Complete 500–700-word critical analysis
4. Revised annotated bibliography with all eight core sources
5. A text version of the poster content and descriptions of the poster images

Name the file:

`P1_Multimodal-Profile-Writing.pdf`

Make the PDF accessible:

- Export it as a text-based PDF, not as scanned pages or screenshots.
- Make sure the text can be selected and searched.
- Use clear headings and a consistent reading order.
- Use a readable font and sufficient contrast between text and background.
- Keep links clickable and give them descriptive names.

#### Identify an artistic direction

Identify one strategy, method, question, or tension from the selected artist or collective that could inform an original artwork.

Your proposal should enter into a critical dialogue with the artist’s practice. Do not copy the appearance of an existing artwork. Consider what you might adapt, question, translate, combine, or challenge through your own approach.

#### Write the concept statement

Write a **150–200-word concept statement** explaining:

- the main idea or question for the proposed work;
- its connection to the working thesis and critical analysis;
- the strategy, method, or concern drawn from the artist or collective;
- how your approach will differ from the artist’s work; and
- the EDI or anti-oppression issue the project will address.

Use clear and specific language. Explain what you are proposing rather than relying on broad statements about awareness, diversity, or social change.

#### Create a visual development page

Create one visual development page showing how the idea might take form. You may use:

- a mind map;
- concept sketches;
- a storyboard;
- a process or interaction diagram;
- a digital or physical collage; or
- a combination of these formats.

The page must show the development of the idea rather than function only as decoration. Add short labels or annotations that explain important choices and connections.

Make the page accessible:

- use readable text and strong contrast;
- do not communicate information through colour alone;
- add captions and credits for images from other sources; and
- include a text explanation of the page’s organization, images, and key ideas in the PDF.

#### Prepare the proposal PDF

Submit one PDF per pair containing:

1. Both partners’ full names and student numbers
2. Title of the proposal
3. Complete 150–200-word concept statement
4. Annotated visual development page
5. A text explanation of the visual development page
6. Captions and credits for images from other sources

Name the file:

`P1_Multimodal-Profile-Proposal.pdf`

Export a text-based PDF with selectable text, a clear reading order, and sufficient contrast. Do not submit scanned pages or screenshots as the complete PDF.

#### Design the research poster

Create a digitally designed research poster that communicates the main direction of the profile. It should provide enough information to understand the project without reproducing the complete written analysis.

The poster must include:

- artist or collective name;
- a brief introduction to the artist or collective;
- main research question;
- working thesis;
- key evidence or ideas from the course readings and research;
- both selected artworks with images and short captions;
- a brief concept statement for the research-creation proposal with a representative image; and
- selected APA references.

Use approximately **300–400 words**, excluding captions, credits, and references.

#### Follow the poster design requirements

- Create the poster digitally.
- Use an 18 × 24-inch vertical format.
- Use full colour unless the visual approach requires otherwise.
- Organize the content using clear headings, short sections, and a logical reading order.
- Use readable type sizes and strong contrast between text and background.
- Do not communicate meaning through colour alone.
- Do not place important text over visually complex images.
- Balance text, images, and negative space.
- Use a consistent grid, alignment, colour palette, and typographic system.
- Make the main argument and artworks visible from a reasonable viewing distance.
- Include a title, year, caption, and credit for every artwork image.

Avoid filling the poster with long paragraphs or using images without explanation. A viewer should be able to identify the artist, argument, artworks, concepts, and research direction within a few minutes.

#### Prepare for the poster session

- Print the poster at 18 × 24 inches before the poster session.
- Check the printed poster for legibility, colour, image quality, and cropping.
- Bring the printed poster to class.
- Both partners must be prepared to explain the project and respond to questions.

If printing or participating in the poster session creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an alternative that meets the same learning goals.

#### Prepare the poster PDF

Export the poster as a print-ready PDF at its final 18 × 24-inch size.

Name the file:

`P1_Multimodal-Profile-Poster.pdf`

When possible, export the poster with selectable text rather than flattening the entire design into one image. The writing PDF must also contain a text version of the poster content and descriptions of its images.

You may also export a high-quality `.JPG` or `.PNG` preview for your own records, but only the PDF is required for submission.
