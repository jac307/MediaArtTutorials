# P1: Critical Research-Creation Profile · Pairs

## Overview

In pairs, you will **research an artist or collective whose practice addresses issues of equity, diversity, and inclusion**. Using course readings, academic research, artist sources, and selected artworks, you will develop a multimodal profile for the course website.

**The project is completed through four versions**. Each version builds on the previous one by moving from initial research to critical analysis, a preliminary research-creation proposal, a poster session, and the final Webador profile.

## Project development

| Version | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Version 1: Research Direction** | Select an artist or collective, establish the research focus and questions, identify artworks, and map the initial sources. |
| **Version 2: Critical Connections** | Examine the sources and artworks, develop a working thesis and critical-connections outline, and prepare the profile materials and images. |
| **Version 3: Multimodal Profile Draft** | Write the critical analysis, develop a preliminary research-creation proposal, and design a research poster. |
| **Version 4: Revised Profile Presentation** | Present the research, discuss possible research-creation directions, and learn from the profiles developed by other pairs. |
| **Version 5: Final Profile** | Revise and publish the complete profile on Webador and submit the final project package. |

> Each version is part of the project process. Keep all research notes, source information, writing, images, captions, and links organized so they can be revised and reused in later versions.

## Research and writing expectations

- Read, watch, or listen to the assigned sources in full.
- Examine the artworks directly through reliable images, video, audio, or other documentation.
- Write notes, annotations, descriptions, and analysis in your own words.
- Use specific evidence rather than broad claims about the artist or community.
- Distinguish the artist’s statements from your interpretation and from other writers’ arguments.
- Use APA citations for borrowed ideas, information, and quotations.
- Do not reduce an artist or community to trauma, discrimination, or identity categories.
- Consider representation, consent, access, positionality, cultural context, and accountability.
- Use only images and media suitable for publication on the public course website.

Your profile will include:

- An introduction to the artist or collective
- Two selected artworks or projects
- Research questions, a thesis, and two central concepts
- A 500–700-word critical analysis
- An annotated bibliography with eight core sources
- A preliminary research-creation proposal
- Images, captions, credits, links, and alt text
- A research poster

> The preliminary research-creation proposal may inform Project 2, but it does not commit you to a final artistic direction.
