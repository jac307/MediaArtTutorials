# P2: Research-Creation Project

## Prototype Critique and Feedback Session

**Work format:** Groups of 3–4 and whole-class participation

**Goal:** Present **Artistic Prototype: Version 2**, examine how the project communicates its artistic and research direction, and gather specific feedback for the next stage of development.

Each group will briefly introduce its project, present an excerpt or demonstration of the prototype, and participate in a structured feedback discussion. The rest of the class will contribute advice, comments, questions, and perspectives through spoken, written, or approved digital feedback.

> This is a critique of work in progress. The purpose is to identify what is effective, what remains unclear or underdeveloped, and what the group should prioritize next.

## Session schedule

Each of the 10 projects will receive one **15-minute time block**.

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Introduce the project using the slides | 1 minute |
| Present the prototype excerpt, demonstration, or guided experience | Up to 4 minutes |
| Audience records individual feedback | 2 minutes |
| Class feedback and discussion | 6 minutes |
| Change computers and prepare the next project | 2 minutes |
| **Total per project** | **15 minutes** |

The three-hour class will be organized as follows:

| Session activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Opening instructions and setup | 10 minutes |
| Projects 1–5 | 75 minutes |
| Break | 10 minutes |
| Projects 6–10 | 75 minutes |
| Final discussion and next steps | 10 minutes |
| **Total** | **180 minutes** |

The schedule depends on every group being ready before its assigned time. Presentation time cannot be extended when setup takes too long.

## Assign the group roles

Assign the roles before class.

| Role | Number of members | Responsibilities |
|---|---:|---|
| Presenters | 2 | Introduce the project, manage the slides, present or demonstrate the prototype, and respond to questions |
| Feedback note-takers | 1–2 | Record audience responses, advice, questions, disagreements, and proposed changes during the discussion |

For groups of three, assign **two presenters and one note-taker**. For groups of four, assign **two presenters and two note-takers**.

If a role creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance. The group may use an equivalent role or communication method that meets the same learning goals.

### Presenter 1

Presenter 1 should:

- Deliver or lead the one-minute introduction
- Identify the artistic question or EDI concern
- Explain the connection to the selected artist or collective and research
- Introduce the question on which the group particularly wants feedback
- Advance the slides or coordinate them with the second presenter

### Presenter 2

Presenter 2 should:

- Introduce the prototype excerpt, demonstration, or guided experience
- Provide only the instructions required to experience the work safely and as intended
- Operate, perform, or coordinate the prototype
- Briefly explain what happens outside the excerpt when necessary
- Help respond to questions during the discussion

### Feedback note-taker or note-takers

The note-takers should:

- Record feedback throughout the discussion
- Separate observations from recommendations
- Note where several audience members express similar or conflicting responses
- Record questions the group could not answer immediately
- Identify suggested changes connected to the research, audience experience, technology, accessibility, ethics, or production
- Confirm important points with the audience when clarification is needed
- Organize and share the notes with the complete group after class

In groups with two note-takers, divide the work:

- One records comments about concept, research, artist connection, and audience interpretation.
- One records comments about structure, technology, accessibility, presentation, and production.

Note-takers may participate in the discussion, but recording the feedback remains their main responsibility.

> All group members remain responsible for understanding the complete project and the feedback received. Assigned roles organize the session; they do not divide ownership of the work.

## Prepare the critique slides

Create a presentation containing **no more than three slides**. The slides should provide context for experiencing the prototype without reproducing the complete findings PDF.

### Slide 1: Project direction

Include:

- Project working title
- Full names of all group members
- Selected artist or collective
- Main artistic question or EDI concern
- One- or two-sentence explanation of the current project direction
- One representative image

### Slide 2: Research and artistic connections

Include:

- Two or three important ideas from the course readings or additional research
- Specific strategies drawn from, translated, questioned, or extended from the selected artist or collective
- One referenced artwork with its title, year, caption, and credit
- The artistic role of the unfamiliar technology or technique

Do not provide a general biography of the artist or collective. Focus on the research and artistic strategies that directly shaped the prototype.

### Slide 3: Artistic Prototype: Version 2

Include:

- One or two images showing the prototype
- One important finding from the earlier audience test
- The main changes made between Prototype Versions 1 and 2
- One element that the group still needs to develop
- One question on which the group would like feedback
- A short link or QR code to the prototype when relevant

## Follow the slide requirements

- Use a 16:9 format, preferably `1920 × 1080 px`.
- Use no more than three slides.
- Keep the text concise and readable.
- Use clear headings and a logical reading order.
- Use strong contrast between text and background.
- Do not communicate meaning through colour alone.
- Do not place important text over visually complex images.
- Use a consistent grid, alignment, colour palette, and typographic system.
- Caption and credit all artwork and external images.
- Add credits for prototype images when required.
- Add useful alt text or a brief image description for important images.
- Provide the complete slide text, image descriptions, captions, and credits in speaker notes or a separate accessible text document.
- Test all links and QR codes before class.
- Do not include student numbers on the slides.

The audience should be able to identify the project, artistic question, artist connection, research foundation, technology, and current prototype direction without reading the complete findings PDF.

## Prepare the one-minute introduction

The two presenters have a combined total of **one minute** to introduce the project.

The introduction should identify:

- The project’s artistic question or EDI concern
- The selected artist or collective
- The main research connection
- What the group wants the audience to notice or consider
- The question on which the group would most like feedback

Plan and rehearse the introduction. Do not read large amounts of text from the slides or use the introduction to tell the audience how it must interpret the artwork.

## Prepare the prototype excerpt or demonstration

The presenters have up to **four minutes** to share an excerpt, demonstration, or guided experience of Prototype Version 2.

If the complete work is longer than four minutes, select an excerpt that demonstrates:

- The project’s central artistic direction
- The connection between the research and artistic choices
- The unfamiliar technology or technique in use
- The intended audience experience
- A significant change made after testing Prototype Version 1

Briefly explain what happens before or after the excerpt when that information is necessary to understand the complete structure.

For installations, interactive works, games, websites, physical works, or projects that cannot be presented as a timed excerpt, prepare a focused demonstration or guided experience lasting no more than four minutes.

Videos must include captions. Audio-only material must include a transcript or an equivalent text summary. Provide a text explanation for important visual, interactive, or spatial information that is not communicated through speech or sound.

> The excerpt or demonstration should represent the prototype accurately. Do not create a separate presentation version that conceals unresolved problems in the work.

## Prepare the group’s computer and prototype

Groups will normally present from their own computers. Before class:

- Save the slides and prototype files on the computer the group will use.
- Open the slides and prototype before the session begins.
- Test all media, software, links, interactions, and required accounts.
- Bring required equipment, materials, cables, adapters, controllers, speakers, and supports.
- Download essential online media when possible.
- Prepare a stable backup copy of the slides and prototype.
- Prepare any necessary audience instructions and accessibility supports.
- Confirm who will carry, connect, and disconnect the computer and equipment.
- Practise moving from the slides to the prototype without unnecessary delay.

When the previous group begins its feedback discussion, the next group should quietly confirm that its computer and materials are ready. Use the two-minute transition to connect the computer and open the prepared presentation.

> Do not depend on downloading, signing in, locating files, editing slides, or installing software during the transition.

If using or transporting a personal computer creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange another presentation setup.

## Present the project

During the presentation:

- Stay within the one-minute introduction and four-minute prototype limits.
- Provide only the instructions necessary to experience the work safely and as intended.
- Allow the prototype to communicate before adding further explanation.
- Clearly indicate when the excerpt or demonstration has ended.
- Move into the feedback stage without beginning a second presentation.

If presenting the prototype or participating in the critique creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an alternative that meets the same learning goals.

## Record individual audience responses

After each prototype, the class will have **two minutes** to record initial responses before discussion begins.

Consider:

1. What was clear or effective in the audience experience?
2. What connection to the research or selected artist was visible?
3. What was confusing, disconnected, inaccessible, or underdeveloped?
4. How did the unfamiliar technology or technique contribute to the artwork?
5. What should the group prioritize in the next version?

Record specific observations rather than only general reactions such as “I liked it” or “it was confusing.” Identify the element that produced the response.

## Participate in the feedback discussion

The class will have **six minutes** to discuss each prototype.

Students who are not presenting are expected to participate through spoken, written, or approved digital feedback. Useful contributions include:

- Specific observations about the audience experience
- Advice for strengthening the prototype
- Comments connecting the work to the selected artist, course readings, or other research
- Interpretations or perspectives that the group may not have considered
- Questions about artistic, technical, ethical, cultural, or accessibility decisions
- Constructive disagreement when supported with a clear reason

When giving feedback:

- Address the artwork and artistic decisions rather than making judgments about the group members.
- Refer to a specific moment, image, sound, interaction, material, instruction, or technical behaviour.
- Explain why a choice was effective, unclear, or significant.
- Distinguish between an observation, interpretation, question, and recommendation.
- Avoid presenting personal preference as a universal rule.
- Allow different audience perspectives to coexist.
- Keep comments concise so multiple people can participate.
- Do not repeat a point unless adding a different example or perspective.

Different interpretations are not automatically problems. Consider whether an interpretation is productively supported by the artwork or reveals missing context, unclear structure, or a contradiction between the project’s intentions and audience experience.

## Participation expectations

Active participation is required throughout the session, but it does not require every student to speak during every critique.

Participation includes:

- Arriving on time and remaining present for the complete session
- Giving full attention to each prototype
- Recording feedback during the two-minute response period
- Contributing advice, comments, questions, or perspectives through speaking, writing, or an approved digital method
- Connecting feedback to the work, research, artist or collective, technology, and intended audience experience
- Listening respectfully to classmates
- Making room for others to contribute
- Avoiding unrelated computer or phone use

Students are expected to contribute regularly across the session and engage seriously with all projects. If the schedule, room, sensory conditions, speaking format, note-taking, or another part of the session creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an equivalent participation method.

> Attendance without attentive participation does not meet the expectations of the critique session.

## Receive feedback as the presenting group

During the first part of the discussion, the presenters should listen and allow audience members to explain their responses. Do not immediately defend or correct every interpretation.

After receiving the initial feedback, group members may:

- Ask for clarification
- Explain a relevant artistic or technical decision
- Identify feedback that confirms or challenges earlier findings
- Ask follow-up questions about a specific audience response
- Discuss possible revisions with the class

The group should not use the discussion to provide a longer presentation of information that was absent from the prototype.

## Organize the feedback after the session

After class, the note-takers must organize the feedback under the following headings:

- Elements to retain
- Audience interpretations and perspectives
- Research and artist connections
- Structure and coherence
- Technology and production
- Accessibility, ethics, consent, or representation
- Recommended revisions
- Questions requiring further investigation

As a group, review the notes and distinguish among:

- Feedback that confirms an existing decision
- Feedback that identifies a necessary revision
- Feedback that offers a possible direction
- Conflicting audience responses requiring further testing
- Feedback the group will not follow, with a clear reason

Keep these notes for the next stage of Project 2.

## Final preparation checklist

Before class, confirm that:

- [ ] Two presenters and one or two feedback note-takers have been assigned.
- [ ] The presentation contains no more than three slides.
- [ ] The one-minute introduction has been rehearsed.
- [ ] The prototype excerpt or demonstration does not exceed four minutes.
- [ ] The group’s feedback question is included.
- [ ] The slides have a text-based alternative containing the slide text, image descriptions, captions, and credits.
- [ ] Slides and prototype files are saved and open on the group’s computer.
- [ ] All media, software, links, interactions, and accounts have been tested.
- [ ] Required equipment, cables, adapters, materials, and accessibility supports are ready.
- [ ] A backup copy of the slides and prototype is available.
- [ ] All group members understand their responsibilities.
- [ ] The group is prepared to connect and disconnect its computer within the transition time.
