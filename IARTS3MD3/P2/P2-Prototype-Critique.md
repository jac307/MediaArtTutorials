## P2: Research-Creation Project

# Prototype Critique and Feedback Session

**Work format:** Groups of 3–4 and whole-class participation

**Goal:** Present **Artistic Prototype No.2**, examine how the artwork communicates its artistic and research direction, and gather specific feedback to guide the next stage of development.

Each group will introduce its project and **state the feedback it wants from the class**, then present or provide an opportunity to experience the prototype. Classmates will respond with observations, questions, and constructive suggestions.

> This is a critique of work in progress, not a final exhibition. Different interpretations are welcome. The purpose is to identify what works, what remains unclear or underdeveloped, and what the group should investigate or revise.

## Session timing

Each group should plan for **9 minutes**, with a **minimum planned allocation of 7 minutes** and an **absolute maximum of 13 minutes**. The time used will depend on the artwork and the discussion.

| Activity | Suggested time |
|---|---:|
| Introduce the project, research connections, and feedback request | 2 minutes |
| Present or allow the class to experience the artwork | 5 minutes |
| Questions, comments, and feedback | 3 minutes |
| **Target per group** | **10 minutes** |
| **Absolute maximum per group, including setup and transition** | **13 minutes** |

> Plan enough time for questions and feedback. Do not use the entire block to explain or demonstrate the artwork.

## Division of labour

All members must understand the project, participate in preparation, and review the feedback received. Assign the following session roles:

| Role | Groups of 3 | Groups of 4 | Main responsibility |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Presenters | 2 | 2 | Introduce the project, state the feedback request, and present or facilitate the artwork |
| Feedback note-takers | 1 | 2 | Record audience observations, questions, recommendations, and disagreements |

> Presenters and note-takers may contribute to the discussion, but the assigned roles must be covered. If a role or presentation method creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an equivalent way to participate.

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

#### <span class="step-label">Step 1:</span> Review Artistic Prototype No.2

Before class, review:

- **Iteration 3 - Revised Proposal**
- **Version 2: First Artistic Prototype**
- **Version 3: Second Artistic Prototype**
- Audience-testing findings and revisions completed in Version 3
- Relevant artistic and academic research
- Specific strategies drawn from, questioned, or extended from the selected artist or collective
- Remaining ethical, accessibility, technical, or production concerns

Discuss what the current prototype communicates through its form, materials, technology, structure, and audience experience.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 2:</span> Decide how the class will experience the artwork

Choose an excerpt, demonstration, guided interaction, performance, or another presentation method that allows the class to experience the artwork meaningfully.

Plan for **approximately five minutes**, adapting the experience to the medium and the group's total time allocation.

The experience should demonstrate:

- The main artistic question or EDI concern
- A meaningful connection to the research and selected artist or collective
- How the unfamiliar technology or technique functions **within the artwork**
- The intended audience experience
- A significant change made after testing Prototype No.1

> If the complete artwork is longer than the available time, select a representative excerpt and briefly explain what happens outside it. For physical, spatial, installation, or interactive projects, prepare a feasible demonstration or guided encounter.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 3:</span> Identify the feedback your group needs

Agree on **one or two specific questions** that the group wants the class to address.

Focus on an artistic decision, audience experience, research connection, or unresolved issue that can still be investigated or revised.

**Instructor-created example:**

> "Does the change in projected imagery make the relationship between visitor movement and surveillance clear, or does it read mainly as a visual effect?"

Avoid general questions such as "Did you like it?" or "Is it good?"

**The presenters must state the feedback question(s) during the project introduction, before presenting the artwork.**

#### <span class="step-label">Step 4:</span> Assign the group roles

Assign the following responsibilities before class:

| Role                                     | Responsibility                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Two presenters**                       | Introduce the project, present the slides, state the feedback questions, and demonstrate or coordinate the artwork experience |
| **One or two feedback note-takers**      | Record audience observations, questions, recommendations, and disagreements                                                   |
| **Technical and setup responsibilities** | Prepare equipment, media, materials, access instructions, and the presentation space                                          |

A group of three will have two presenters and one note-taker. A group of four will have two presenters and two note-takers.

Technical and setup responsibilities may be shared among these members.

All members must understand the research, current prototype, and feedback questions. If an assigned role creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an equivalent participation method.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 5:</span> Prepare the three-slide presentation

Create **no more than three slides**. The slides should provide context for experiencing the artwork, not reproduce the group findings PDF.

| Slide                                    | Required content                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Project direction**                 | Working title, names of all members, selected artist or collective, main artistic question or EDI concern, concise explanation of the current direction, and one representative image                                                 |
| **2. Research and artistic connections** | Two or three relevant research ideas, specific artistic strategies translated, questioned, or extended, one referenced artwork with title, year, caption, and credit, and the artistic role of the unfamiliar technology or technique |
| **3. Artistic Prototype No.2**           | One or two prototype images, an important audience-testing finding, main changes since Prototype No.1, one unresolved element, and **the specific feedback question(s)**                                                              |

Include a short link or QR code to the prototype when useful.

**Slide and accessibility requirements:**

- Use a 16:9 slide format.
- Keep text concise and readable.
- Organize information with clear headings and a logical reading order.
- Use sufficient contrast between text and background.
- Do not communicate meaning through colour alone.
- Avoid placing important text over visually complex images.
- Include captions and credits for artwork and external images.
- Provide useful alt text or equivalent descriptions for important images.
- Make the complete slide text, image descriptions, captions, and credits available in speaker notes or a separate accessible text document.
- Test links and QR codes before class.
- Do not include student numbers on the slides.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 6:</span> Rehearse the introduction and artwork experience

Plan for a **10-minute target** for the complete group session:

| Activity                                    |    Target time |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------: |
| Project introduction and feedback request   |      2 minutes |
| Artwork presentation or audience experience |      5 minutes |
| Questions and feedback                      |      3 minutes |
| **Total target**                            | **10 minutes** |

The minimum planned allocation is **8 minutes**, and the absolute maximum is **15 minutes**, including questions and feedback.

During the introduction, the presenters must:

1. Identify the artistic question or EDI concern.
2. Explain the connection to the selected artist or collective and research.
3. Briefly introduce the prototype and how the audience will experience it.
4. **State the exact feedback question(s) the group wants the class to address.**

Do not tell the audience how they must interpret the artwork. Provide the context and instructions necessary to experience it safely and as intended.

Rehearse the excerpt, demonstration, performance, or guided interaction. Confirm that the class will have an opportunity to experience the artwork rather than only hear a description of it.

#### Before class: Final checklist

- [ ] We reviewed Artistic Prototype No.2 and the previous research and testing findings.
- [ ] We selected a meaningful excerpt, demonstration, or audience experience.
- [ ] We agreed on one or two specific feedback questions.
- [ ] We assigned two presenters and one or two feedback note-takers.
- [ ] We prepared no more than three slides.
- [ ] Our slides include the required research connections, images, credits, and accessible text alternatives.
- [ ] We rehearsed the introduction, including the feedback request.
- [ ] We rehearsed the artwork experience and planned for the 10-minute target.
- [ ] Artistic Prototype No.2 is ready to present or experience.
- [ ] All required equipment, files, materials, and accessibility supports are prepared.
- [ ] We tested the presentation and prepared a backup.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 1:</span> Prepare the computer, materials, and backup

**You must bring Artistic Prototype No.2 and everything required to present or experience it in class.**

- Save the slides and prototype files on the computer the group will use.
- Open and test the slides, media, software, links, interactions, and accounts.
- Bring required equipment, materials, cables, adapters, controllers, and other supports.
- Prepare a stable backup of essential slides and prototype files.
- Prepare instructions and accessibility supports for audience participation.
- Confirm who will connect, operate, and disconnect equipment.
- Practise changing from the slides to the artwork without unnecessary delay.

Groups will normally use their own computers. If using or transporting a personal computer creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an alternative setup.

Do not depend on downloading files, signing into new accounts, installing software, or editing the prototype during the transition.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 2:</span> Introduce the project and feedback request

In approximately **two minutes**, identify the project, its artistic and research direction, and **what feedback the group wants from the class**.

Make the request specific enough that classmates can respond with observations from the artwork.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 3:</span> Present or facilitate the artwork experience

In approximately **five minutes**, present an excerpt, demonstration, performance, or guided experience of **Artistic Prototype: Version 2**.

Provide necessary safety and access instructions. Let the artistic choices communicate through the work. Explain briefly what is not included in the excerpt only when essential.

Clearly indicate when the experience ends and the feedback discussion begins.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 4:</span> Receive questions and feedback

Allow approximately **three minutes** for audience questions, comments, and responses. The presenting group should listen before responding, ask for clarification when useful, and avoid using this time for a second presentation.

The note-taker or note-takers should record:

- Specific observations and audience interpretations
- Comments about the research and artist connection
- Responses to the group's feedback question
- Questions, suggestions, and constructive disagreements
- Ethical, accessibility, technological, or production concerns
- Possible revisions and unresolved issues

The instructor will manage the session timing. **Eight minutes is the minimum planned allocation, 10 minutes is the target, and 15 minutes is the absolute maximum, including setup and transition.**

> **Before moving on:** The group has presented the prototype, explicitly requested feedback, allowed time for discussion, and recorded the responses.

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

#### <span class="step-label">Step 1:</span> Experience and respond to other projects

Students who are not presenting should attend to the artwork and contribute through spoken, written, or approved digital feedback.

Consider:

1. What moment, image, sound, interaction, or material shaped your interpretation?
2. Which research or artist connection was visible in the artwork?
3. What was clear, unexpected, confusing, inaccessible, or underdeveloped?
4. How did the unfamiliar technology or technique contribute to the artistic experience?
5. What response can you offer to the group's **specific feedback question**?
6. What change or further test might strengthen the work?

Give concrete observations. Rather than saying only “it was confusing,” identify the element that produced the confusion and explain why.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 2:</span> Contribute constructively

- Discuss the artwork and artistic decisions, not the personal qualities of the group.
- Distinguish observations, interpretations, questions, and recommendations.
- Explain your reasoning and refer to specific evidence.
- Avoid treating personal preference as a universal rule.
- Make room for different perspectives and participation methods.
- Keep comments concise so more classmates can contribute.
- Do not assume a different interpretation is automatically a failure.

Active participation does not require every student to speak during every critique. Students are expected to contribute regularly and engage seriously with the projects. If a participation format creates an access barrier, contact the instructor in advance to arrange an equivalent method.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 3:</span> Organize the group's feedback after class

The note-takers should share their notes with all group members. Organize the responses under:

- Elements to retain
- Audience interpretations and perspectives
- Research and artist connections
- Structure and coherence
- Technology and production
- Accessibility, ethics, consent, and representation
- Recommended revisions
- Questions requiring further investigation

As a group, identify feedback that confirms an existing decision, indicates a necessary revision, suggests a possible direction, or calls for further testing. Record the reason when the group decides not to follow a recommendation.

Keep these notes for the next stage of Project 2.

> **Before moving on:** The group has reviewed the feedback together and identified which observations or questions should inform its next revisions.

## Final preparation checklist

Before the critique session, confirm that:

- [ ] The group has read the complete critique instructions.
- [ ] Two presenters and one or two feedback note-takers are assigned.
- [ ] **Artistic Prototype: Version 2** is ready to bring and experience in class.
- [ ] The presentation has no more than three slides.
- [ ] The introduction identifies the artistic direction, research connection, and **specific feedback requested**.
- [ ] The group has planned approximately 2 minutes for the introduction, 5 minutes for the artwork, and 3 minutes for questions and feedback.
- [ ] The group can work within the 8–15-minute allocation, with **15 minutes as the absolute maximum including setup and transition**.
- [ ] The prototype presentation accurately represents the work.
- [ ] Slide text, captions, credits, and important visual descriptions are available in an accessible format.
- [ ] Captions, transcripts, and other relevant access supports are prepared.
- [ ] Slides, prototype files, media, software, links, and accounts have been tested.
- [ ] Required equipment, materials, cables, and adapters are ready.
- [ ] A backup copy of essential files is available.
- [ ] All group members understand their roles and the feedback question.

**No separate submission is required for this session unless the instructor specifies otherwise.** Bring the slides, prototype, and required materials to class, and keep the feedback notes for the next stage.
