## P2: Research-Creation Project

# Version 3: Audience Testing and Prototype Development

**Work format:** Groups of 3–4

**Submission:** Each group submits one group findings PDF through Avenue to Learn. Each group also completes the **Version 3: Second Artistic Prototype** section on Webador.

**Important:** Read the complete [Prototype Critique and Feedback Session](P2-Prototype-Critique.md) instructions in preparation for next class.

## Goal

Analyze the first artistic prototype from the audience’s perspective, determine whether the artistic decisions are clear and supported by the research, and identify the changes required for the next version. Use the findings to produce **Version 3: Second Artistic Prototype**, ready to share during the prototype critique.

> This is still a work-in-progress stage. Prototype No.2 must contain the required materials and replace the placeholders used in the first one, but it may still require refinement before the final submission.

## Division of labour

Every member must contribute to the analysis, revision, production, and preparation of Prototype No.1, by doing the following activities:

| Audience testing or documentation | Analysis responsibility | Production responsibility | Critique-session preparation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assigned task | Assigned area | Required revision or material | Assigned preparation task |

> Dividing the analysis into isolated sections without discussing the complete prototype as a group does not meet the collaborative requirement.

## Read and complete the instructions step by step

**Complete this version in the order presented below.**

Do not try to complete all of the requirements at once. Each stage builds on the work from the previous stage.

**Start with Stage 1 and complete each step before moving to the next stage.** Use the checklists and **Before moving on** prompts to confirm that you have completed the required work.

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

#### <span class="step-label">Step 1:</span> Prepare the first prototype for an audience testing

Before beginning:

- Confirm that the prototype can be opened, played, performed, installed, navigated, or experienced.
- Prepare any instructions that would normally be available to the audience.
- Identify what the group wants to learn from this test.
- Decide who will facilitate, observe, record feedback, and document the session.
- Obtain consent before photographing, recording, quoting, or identifying a participant.
- Do not collect personal information that is unnecessary for the activity.

Ask **at least two people who are not members of the group** to experience the prototype and meet with them together or individually.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 2:</span> Complete the audience test

**Complete one uninterrupted viewing, run-through, or interaction** before asking questions. Do not provide interpretive context or explain the project beforehand. Provide only the instructions necessary to experience the work safely and as intended.

After each participant experiences the work, ask:

1. What did you notice first?
2. How did you understand what to see, hear, do, or follow?
3. What ideas, questions, emotions, or relationships did the work suggest?
4. Which element most strongly shaped your interpretation?
5. Was anything confusing, disconnected, inaccessible, or unnecessary?
6. What would you want to experience more clearly or fully?

> Record the main responses accurately. A brief summary is sufficient; a full transcript is not required.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 3:</span> Document the audience test

Include the following in the group findings PDF:

- Date and format of the test
- Number of participants
- Participants’ relationship to the project, without unnecessary identifying information
- Questions the group wanted to investigate
- Observation notes
- Summary of audience responses

#### <span class="step-label">Step 4:</span> Analyze the prototype and audience findings

Complete the following table using specific evidence and add it to your PDF.

| Area | Intended direction | Evidence from the prototype and audience test | Finding | Required change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concept and EDI concern | What should the work investigate or communicate? | What did participants notice or overlook? | What is clear, unclear, simplified, or contradictory? | Retain, clarify, revise, add, or remove |
| Research connection | Which ideas or contexts inform the project? | Where are they visible in the artistic decisions? | Is the connection meaningful and specific? | Strengthen, clarify, contextualize, or revise |
| Artist or collective connection | Which strategies are translated, questioned, or extended? | Where can these relationships be experienced? | Does the project create a dialogue rather than imitate? | Retain, strengthen, or revise |
| Form and structure | How should the parts work together? | Where did attention, pacing, sequence, or coherence succeed or fail? | What relationships are effective or disconnected? | Reorder, connect, reduce, expand, or revise |
| Audience experience | What should the audience see, hear, encounter, or do? | What happened during the test? | What was understandable, engaging, confusing, or inaccessible? | Clarify instructions, interaction, presentation, or access |
| Technology | What artistic role should the unfamiliar technology perform? | How did it shape the experience? | Did it support the concept or function mainly as an effect? | Retain, integrate, simplify, repair, or revise |
| Ethics and accessibility | What responsibilities shape the work? | What concern or barrier appeared during testing? | What is effective, missing, or risky? | Add or revise an ethical or access measure |
| Materials and production | What is required for the next prototype? | What remains temporary, missing, inconsistent, or unreliable? | What prevents the work from being ready for the prototype critique? | Produce, replace, repair, standardize, or test |

#### <span class="step-label">Step 5:</span> Distinguish revision from interpretation

Do not revise the project simply because one participant interpreted it differently from the group’s intention. Determine whether:

- The interpretation is productively supported by the artwork
- The response reveals a meaningful ambiguity
- The audience lacked necessary information or instructions
- The artistic choices contradict the intended concept
- The research connection exists in the proposal but is not present in the work

> The goal is not to eliminate ambiguity or require one correct interpretation. The goal is to make the project’s structure, context, and artistic decisions intentional enough to support meaningful audience engagement.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 6:</span> Identify the central findings

Before the next stage, identify:

- The three strongest elements to retain
- The three most important problems to address
- Any difference between the group’s intention and the audience’s experience
- Any research connection that needs to become more visible or precise
- Any element that is technically successful but artistically unnecessary
- Any missing context, instruction, or accessibility support
- Any part of the scope that should be reduced rather than rushed

> Support each finding with evidence from the prototype, audience test, or research.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 1:</span> Prioritize the changes

Use the findings from the prototype analysis and audience test to identify the changes required for the second prototype.

Add one row for each change:

| Priority | Finding or problem | Evidence | Required change | Assigned member or members | Deadline | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Required, Important, or Optional | What needs attention? | What evidence supports this finding? | What specific action will be completed? | Full name(s) | Date and time | How will completion be confirmed? |

Use the following priorities:

| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Required | The change is necessary for the concept, audience experience, accessibility, research connection, or prototype to function |
| Important | The change strengthens clarity, coherence, presentation, or the connection between the research and artwork |
| Optional | The change adds refinement if sufficient time remains |

> Complete all **Required** changes before beginning Optional changes.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 2:</span> Assemble the second Prototype

Prototype No.2 must:

- Present the complete current structure of the project
- Include the materials and content required for the intended artwork
- Replace the placeholders used in Prototype No.1
- Integrate the unfamiliar technology or technique into the audience experience
- Demonstrate a clear and specific relationship to the research
- Include the instructions required to encounter or operate the work
- Address the main accessibility, consent, privacy, representation, and safety findings
- Be stable enough to open, play, perform, install, navigate, or experience during the prototype critique

> The prototype may still require refinement in areas such as timing, transitions, visual or sonic treatment, fabrication quality, performance, interface details, or installation design.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 3:</span> Complete an internal readiness check

Before the prototype critique, complete one full internal run-through using the intended presentation conditions as closely as possible.

Confirm that:

- [ ] The prototype has a clear entry point for the audience.
- [ ] Instructions can be understood without a group member explaining every step.
- [ ] The complete sequence, interaction, performance, or audience path can be experienced.
- [ ] All required materials are present and placeholders have been replaced.
- [ ] The connection between the concept, research, artistic strategies, and technology is visible in the work.
- [ ] Required equipment, software, files, accounts, cables, and adapters are available.
- [ ] Media and links open without requesting access.
- [ ] Credits and citations are recorded.
- [ ] Consent and privacy requirements have been addressed.
- [ ] Accessibility supports and alternatives are prepared.
- [ ] A stable copy and backup of the prototype are available.

Record any remaining issue and explain how the group will manage it during the prototype critique.

#### <span class="step-label">Step 4:</span> Document your Prototype No.2

Include clear instructions for accessing Prototype No.2 in the group findings PDF.

> For video, animation, sound, performance, interactive, or other time-based work, upload the prototype documentation to YouTube or Vimeo and provide the accessible link. The media may be unlisted but must remain accessible without requiring the instructor to request permission. Videos must include captions. Audio-only material must include a transcript or an equivalent text summary.

For physical, spatial, installation, or live work, include **three to five images** showing:

- The complete setup
- Important details, materials, screens, or interfaces
- The unfamiliar technology or technique in use
- The intended audience perspective

Each image must include:
- A brief caption
- Alt text
- A credit when required

> Obtain consent before photographing, recording, quoting, or identifying anyone outside the group.
