# P2: Research-Creation Project

**Work format:** Groups of 3–4  
**Final format:** Completed research-creation artwork, public project record on Webador, research-creation poster, and class exhibition

## Overview

In groups of three or four, you will develop a research-creation project connected to an artist or collective studied during Project 1. The project must engage an issue of equity, diversity, and inclusion and use research to inform its artistic form, materials, technology, and intended audience experience.

The project develops through several connected stages. Each stage must build on evidence from the previous one. Research, technological testing, audience responses, and critique should result in visible changes to the project rather than remain separate from the artwork.

## How the project develops

| Stage | Main focus |
|---|---|
| **Version 1: Artist or Collective Inspiration and Concept Proposal** | Revisit the preliminary research-creation direction, establish the group, define the artistic question or EDI concern, connect the concept to the selected artist or collective, and develop the first proposal iterations. |
| **Technological Research** | Learn and test an unfamiliar technology or technique, document individual experimentation, evaluate its artistic possibilities and limitations, and decide whether to continue, modify, or replace it. |
| **Version 2: First Artistic Prototype** | Revise the proposal using the technological findings, divide the production work, combine the components into one coherent rough cut, and document First Artistic Prototype: Version 1. |
| **Version 3: Audience Testing and Prototype Development** | Test the first prototype with people outside the group, compare the audience experience with the group’s intentions and research, prioritize changes, replace placeholders, and produce Artistic Prototype: Version 2. |
| **Prototype Critique and Feedback Session** | Present Prototype Version 2, explain its research and artist connections, receive advice and perspectives from the class, and record feedback for the final revisions. |
| **Final Submission** | Complete and document the final artwork, write the collaborative Artistic Analysis, update the APA references, create the final poster, and review the complete Webador entry. |
| **Class Exhibition** | Install and present the final project, engage with visitors, and contribute to the organization and museography, setup, or cleanup of the shared exhibition. |

Each stage is part of the project process. Keep all research notes, source information, plans, test results, production files, audience responses, writing, images, captions, credits, permissions, and links organized so they can be revised and reused later.

## Research and artistic expectations

- Use the Project 1 research as a foundation while continuing to locate sources required by the developing project.
- Read, watch, or listen to assigned and selected sources in full.
- Examine the selected artist’s or collective’s work through reliable documentation and primary sources when available.
- Use research to inform specific artistic decisions rather than treating it as background information added after production.
- Draw from, translate, question, or extend identifiable strategies used by the selected artist or collective. Do not simply imitate an artwork’s appearance.
- Connect the project’s form, materials, structure, technology, and audience experience to the artistic question or EDI concern.
- Use specific evidence instead of broad claims about an artist, identity, community, culture, or social issue.
- Separate artists’ statements, scholarly arguments, audience responses, and the group’s interpretations.
- Use APA citations for borrowed ideas, information, terminology, and quotations.
- Consider representation, consent, access, positionality, cultural context, privacy, safety, and accountability throughout the project.
- Do not claim that the artwork speaks for a community or solves the EDI concern it examines.

## Accessibility and media expectations

- Organize information using clear headings and a logical reading order.
- Use readable text and strong contrast between text and background.
- Do not communicate important information through colour alone.
- Give links descriptive names instead of using phrases such as “click here.”
- Add a caption, credit, and useful alt text to every image.
- Provide captions for video and a transcript or equivalent text summary for audio-only material.
- Provide a text alternative when important information appears in a poster, diagram, collage, or other image.
- Export documents as text-based PDFs with selectable text and working links. Do not submit scanned pages or screenshots as the complete PDF.

> **Alt text** is a brief text description that communicates an image’s relevant content or purpose to someone who cannot see it.

If a required tool, process, space, presentation format, or exhibition task creates an access barrier, contact the instructor as early as possible. An equivalent method or role can be arranged when it meets the same learning goals.

## What the final project will include

- A complete research-creation artwork
- A complete public Webador entry preserving the proposals, Technological Research, and both prototype versions
- A 150–200-word final project description
- Five to eight final-project documentation images
- Embedded video or audio, or a stable public project link, when required to experience the work
- Captions, credits, permissions, and useful alt text for all required media
- Audience instructions and accessibility information when required
- A collaborative Artistic Analysis containing a 150–200-word introduction, one 150–200-word decision paragraph written by each member, and a 150–200-word conclusion
- A final APA reference list without annotations
- A digital 18 × 24-inch research-creation poster uploaded to Webador
- A QR code on the poster linking to the complete public Webador entry
- One printed 18 × 24-inch poster delivered to the instructor
- Installation and presentation of the final artwork and poster in the Class Exhibition
- Participation in the Organization and Museography, Setup, or Cleanup Team

The final project should demonstrate how research, experimentation, audience testing, critique, and collaboration shaped the completed artwork.
