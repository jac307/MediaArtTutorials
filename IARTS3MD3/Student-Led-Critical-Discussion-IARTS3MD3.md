# Student-Led Critical Discussion

## Team activity · 3 students

**Goal:** Lead a focused **40-minute class session** that connects the two assigned readings with the assigned artist or collective. Each team will give a 20-minute presentation followed by a 20-minute non-digital discussion activity inspired by the artist's work, questions, methods, or materials.

## Activity overview

- **Group size:** Three students
- **Format:** In-person presentation and facilitated discussion activity
- **Assessment:** 7 points
- **Equipment:** Use your own computer for the presentation; HDMI adapters will be available
- **Submission:** Slides, activity plan, and printable materials are due one day before the presentation

> **Hard time limit:** The full session ends at **40:00**. The instructor will stop the session at that point. Rehearse both parts together and leave enough time for participants to contribute during the activity.

## Enrolment process

1. Form a team of three students.
2. Review the available sets of readings and assigned artists or collectives.
3. Select one available set and register through the enrolment link on Avenue to Learn.
4. Confirm that all three team members are enrolled in the correct group.
5. Read both assigned texts closely.
6. Research the assigned artist or collective and examine the assigned artwork, series, or project.
7. Divide the research, presentation, activity preparation, and facilitation responsibilities across the team.

All team members must contribute meaningfully to the research, presentation, and discussion activity.

## Required timing

| Stage | Time |
|---|---:|
| Presentation: readings, artist, and selected connections | 20 minutes |
| Non-digital discussion activity | 20 minutes |
| **Total** | **40 minutes** |

## Primary-material requirement

The two assigned readings and the assigned artist or collective must be the primary materials for both parts of the session.

Additional sources or artistic examples may be used when they clarify a concept or provide necessary context. They must remain secondary and must not replace sustained engagement with the assigned materials.

## Presentation purpose

The presentation is not an exhaustive summary of both readings. Its purpose is to identify **two or three central concepts, arguments, or points of tension** and use the assigned artist's work to explain, test, or complicate them.

The main section should integrate the readings and artwork rather than presenting three disconnected summaries.

## Introduce the readings and authors

Briefly identify:

- The authors and titles of both readings
- The context and general focus of each text
- Why the readings matter for this week's course theme
- A key point of agreement, difference, or tension between them

Keep this section concise. Do not provide extended biographies or summarize every section of either reading.

## Introduce the artist or collective

Briefly identify:

- The artist or collective
- Relevant cultural, social, political, geographical, or historical context
- The assigned artwork, series, or project
- The medium, materials, technologies, processes, site, audience, or collaborative structure
- The central concerns of the work

Use clear, high-quality documentation. Include the title, year, medium, and source for every image or media excerpt when that information is available.

## Select two or three central ideas

Choose **two or three ideas** that help the class understand meaningful relationships among both readings and the artist's work.

For each idea:

1. State the concept or argument clearly.
2. Explain it in your own words.
3. Refer to a specific passage or example from at least one reading.
4. Explain how the second reading supports, extends, complicates, or challenges it.
5. Connect the idea to a specific artwork, detail, method, material, technology, question, or decision by the assigned artist.
6. Explain why this connection matters for artistic practice, research-creation, or anti-oppression work in the arts.

The selected ideas do not need to represent everything in the readings. Prioritize depth, synthesis, and meaningful connections.

## Organize the presentation around connections

Organize the main section around direct connections:

- **Connection 1:** Concept from the readings + specific artistic evidence
- **Connection 2:** Concept from the readings + specific artistic evidence
- **Connection 3, if used:** Tension, limitation, or further question + specific artistic evidence

Artistic evidence may include:

- Materials and technologies
- Process and production methods
- Image, sound, movement, language, or performance
- Site, scale, duration, and audience experience
- Collaboration and participation
- Representation and self-representation
- Cultural, political, or historical context
- Consent, access, care, refusal, or accountability
- Distribution, documentation, or public engagement

Avoid unsupported statements. Identify where an idea appears in the work, how the artistic decision operates, and what it communicates or makes possible.

## Invite engagement during the presentation

You may include a small number of open questions during the presentation. These questions should help the class notice an important detail, consider a tension between the readings, or prepare for the discussion activity.

Keep these exchanges brief so that the presentation remains within 20 minutes.

## Conclude with key takeaways

End by identifying two or three concise takeaways:

- What should the class remember from the readings?
- What does the artist's work help us understand or question?
- How might these ideas inform research-creation or anti-oppression strategies in artistic practice?

## Presentation requirements

- Present in person for exactly 20 minutes.
- Use slides and appropriate images, audio, or video excerpts.
- Use the assigned artist's work as the primary artistic evidence.
- Keep added examples secondary.
- Cite all readings, artworks, images, media, and additional sources.
- Organize speaking responsibilities across all team members.
- Use clear language, readable slides, and appropriate content notices when needed.
- Test all presentation media before class.

## Activity purpose

Design one structured activity that invites the class to engage more deeply with the selected concepts and the assigned artist's ideas or approaches.

The activity should be **inspired by the artist**, not simply followed by questions about the artist. It may adapt:

- A question the artist asks
- A method or process used in the work
- A material or physical format
- A strategy of participation, testimony, care, refusal, translation, or collaboration
- A way of organizing images, language, sound, bodies, objects, space, or audiences
- A tension or ethical problem raised by the work

Students might respond to a question used by the artist, analyze the artist's choices, work with comparable physical materials, reinterpret a process, take positions on a dilemma, or collectively examine how meaning changes through different decisions.

> Translate the artist's strategy into an appropriate classroom activity. Do not copy the artwork or reproduce culturally specific, sacred, personal, or community-held content.

## Non-digital requirement

The activity must be completed through in-person conversation and physical participation.

Participants may not be required to use:

- Cellphones
- Laptops
- Tablets
- Online polls
- Shared digital documents
- Websites or apps
- Digital drawing, editing, or production software

The team may project:

- The activity instructions
- Discussion questions
- A timer
- Images or details from the assigned artwork when they are necessary references

Projection should support the activity, not replace participation. The activity itself must use conversation, printed materials, writing, drawing, collage, movement, objects, spatial arrangements, or other non-digital methods.

> The presenting team is responsible for preparing and bringing all required materials.

## Activity requirements

The activity must:

- Have one clear critical purpose
- Be directly connected to both readings and the assigned artist
- Apply at least one of the presentation's selected concepts
- Be meaningfully inspired by the artist's questions, methods, materials, or approaches
- Include concise instructions and realistic timing
- Invite interpretation, comparison, analysis, making, reflection, or debate
- Allow multiple supported responses rather than one correct answer
- Include time for participants to share and respond to one another
- Include follow-up questions that deepen the conversation
- Fit within exactly 20 minutes, including instructions and closing synthesis

## Prepare facilitation questions

Prepare **three to five open-ended questions**, including follow-up prompts.

Strong questions ask participants to:

- Use a concept or passage from one or both readings
- Refer to specific evidence from the artist's work
- Compare the two authors' perspectives
- Explain an interpretation or artistic decision
- Consider cultural context, positionality, power, or accountability
- Identify what the artwork makes visible, refuses, transforms, or leaves unresolved
- Connect analysis with research-creation or their own artistic practice

Avoid questions that can be answered with only “yes,” “no,” a definition, or an unsupported personal opinion.

## Plan inclusive and ethical participation

Use a structure that gives participants time and more than one way to contribute, such as:

- Individual reflection followed by paired or small-group discussion
- Written or drawn responses before speaking
- Physical sorting or arrangement followed by explanation
- Assigned roles within small groups
- Position-taking with the option to pass or remain undecided
- Whole-class synthesis after small-group work
- Contributions through writing, speaking, making, or observation

Do not require participants to:

- Speak on behalf of a community or identity group
- Simulate a disability or marginalized identity
- Re-enact violence, discrimination, or traumatic events
- Touch other participants or be photographed or recorded without explicit consent

Provide an alternative way to participate when the activity involves movement, speaking publicly, sensory materials, or potentially sensitive content.

## Facilitation responsibilities

The team must:

- Explain the purpose and instructions clearly.
- Distribute and collect materials efficiently.
- Track the time.
- Invite participation without forcing disclosure.
- Ask follow-up questions rather than moving immediately to the next prompt.
- Encourage participants to use the readings and artwork as evidence.
- Connect different responses to one another.
- Redirect the discussion respectfully when it moves away from the assigned concepts.
- Address disagreement without treating one interpretation as automatically correct.
- Conclude by summarizing what the activity helped the class understand, question, or reconsider.

## Suggested activity timing

| Stage | Time |
|---|---:|
| Explain the purpose, instructions, and participation options | 2–3 minutes |
| Individual, paired, or small-group activity | 7–8 minutes |
| Facilitated sharing and critical discussion | 7–8 minutes |
| Summarize connections and unresolved questions | 2 minutes |
| **Total** | **20 minutes** |

## Submission deadline

Each team must submit all materials **at least one day before the scheduled presentation**.

## Submit

- Presentation slides as a PDF: `W#-Presentation.pdf`
- Completed discussion activity plan as a PDF: `W#-Discussion-Activity.pdf`
- Printable activity materials or handouts as PDFs
- Links to any audiovisual excerpts included in the presentation

> Replace `W#` with the assigned week number.

The discussion activity plan must identify:

- The activity's purpose
- The connections to both readings and the artist
- Step-by-step instructions
- Required materials
- Timing for each stage
- Responsibilities for each team member
- Three to five facilitation questions
- Participation alternatives and any relevant content or access considerations
- A backup plan if the activity takes less or more time than expected

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Points | Expectations |
|---|---:|---|
| **Reading synthesis and selected concepts** | 1.5 | Accurately explains two or three central ideas and makes meaningful connections, distinctions, or tensions between both readings without attempting an exhaustive summary. |
| **Artist research and critical connections** | 2.0 | Uses specific works, details, methods, and contextual evidence to explain how the artist supports, complicates, challenges, or extends the selected concepts. |
| **Discussion activity design** | 1.5 | Creates a purposeful non-digital activity that is clearly inspired by the artist and deepens critical engagement with the readings and artwork. Provides clear instructions, appropriate materials, and an accessible structure. |
| **Critical facilitation and participation** | 2.0 | Uses effective questions and follow-up prompts, invites multiple forms of participation, responds constructively to contributions, and concludes with a meaningful synthesis. |
| **Organization, delivery, and collaboration** | 1.0 | Communicates clearly, uses effective slides, shares responsibilities equitably, demonstrates preparation, and respects both 20-minute time limits. |
| **Submission requirements and citations** | 1.0 | Submits all required materials by the deadline using the required formats and filenames, with complete citations and media credits. |
| **Total** | **7.0** | |

> General summaries, disconnected treatment of the readings and artist, unrelated examples, unsupported claims, inaccessible or unclear activity instructions, or exceeding the time limits will weaken the assessment.
